The U.S. Championship is the oldest national chess championship. It began as a series of challenge matches in 1845, and since 1936 the title has been decided by tournament play under the national federation. The Saint Louis Chess Club has hosted it every year since 2009.

The modern event is one of the strongest closed tournaments of the year: a twelve-player field of the country's top grandmasters, with the U.S. Women's Championship played in parallel. Fabiano Caruana has won five titles, four of them in a row from 2022 to 2025; Wesley So ended that run in 2026 with his fourth, beating Caruana in a playoff. Fischer also won four in succession, twice, and only Samuel Reshevsky has five straight titles.

## How it works

**Who plays.** Twelve players qualify by rating, by holding the title, or as winners of the U.S. Junior Championship and the U.S. Open, and the organisers add a wildcard.

**The format.** A single round robin of 11 rounds at classical time control. Since 2014 the event has been a round robin; between 1999 and 2013 it was mostly a larger Swiss.

**What is at stake.** The national title and prize money, plus places in the next FIDE World Cup for the top finishers. A tie for first place is settled by a playoff of rapid games and, if needed, Armageddon, not by tiebreak scores.

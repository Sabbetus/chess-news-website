Daniil Dubov now has the sole lead at the KazChess Masters in Almaty. He [won rounds five and six](https://en.chessbase.com/post/kazchess-masters-almaty-2026-r6) and sits on 4½/6, half a point ahead of Shamsiddin Vokhidov and Denis Lazavik.

Dubov's score is unbeaten: one win and three draws before this pair of victories. For a player with his reputation for sharp, unorthodox play, a quiet start followed by a late surge is a useful shape in a nine-round event.

**A coincidence among the top seeds**

Rounds five and six each produced three decisive games, and the winners were identical both days: the three highest-rated players in the field. Mahammad Muradli (2662), Dubov (2654) and Vokhidov (2649) all won twice in a row.

Their routes there were quite different. Muradli had opened with two losses and two draws, so his back-to-back wins only brought him back to 50 percent.

Vokhidov began with two wins, a draw and a loss, and has climbed to 4/6. His loss came to Lazavik in round four, the game that lifted the Belarusian into a share of the lead at that point.

**Lazavik is the other unbeaten player**

Lazavik is the only player besides Dubov without a defeat. He drew in rounds five and six, which leaves him tied with Vokhidov for second.

Draws kept him close, but they also let Dubov pass him. Lazavik is a half point back, so one decisive result in round seven could change the order again.

**The early leaders slip**

Kazakh players [Edgar Mamedov and Denis Makhnev](/articles/mamedov-16-and-makhnev-lead-the-kazchess-masters-on-2-3/) opened with 2½/3 each. Both drew in round four, and both have since suffered their first defeats.

They stand on 3½/6, a point behind Dubov. That is still within reach with three rounds to play.

Another name worth watching is 14-year-old Mark Smirnov. He won in round four against Sauat Nurgaliyev, his second straight victory.

**What round seven brings**

Sunday's round seven sets all three frontrunners, Dubov, Vokhidov and Lazavik, with the black pieces. It is a small but real hurdle for the top of the table, since Black is rarely the side that presses for a win in a tight finish.

The schedule is compressed from here. Rounds seven and eight are on 1 and 2 November, starting at 11:00 AM CET, and the final round is on 3 November at 7:00 AM CET.

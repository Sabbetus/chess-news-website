GM Hikaru Nakamura of Team Falcons opened the Round of 16 of the Speed Chess Championship XI by [beating 15-year-old GM Yagiz Erdogmus 12-8](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-speed-chess-championship-r16-nakamura-erdogmus). The surprise was where the points came from. Erdogmus won the bullet segment, normally Nakamura's specialty, but the six-point gap Nakamura built in the two blitz segments was too large to close.

**How the segments went**

Nakamura took the 5+1 segment 4.5-2.5 and the 3+1 segment 5.5-1.5. Erdogmus won the 1+1 bullet segment 4-2, which trimmed the final margin to four points.

Chess.com's team gave Nakamura 96-4 odds going in. Erdogmus is the youngest 2700 player in history and was playing his first SCC. Nakamura called him "the most talented junior in the world" and said he knew it wouldn't be easy.

**Why it worked for Nakamura**

His own explanation: "I kept the game very tricky, very tactical until the very end." He was also "just better in the clutch moments" when both players were down to seconds.

He saw an opening weakness as well. "He was a little too set in his openings," Nakamura said, and Erdogmus stuck to the same systems all match.

**Swings in the 5+1**

Nakamura won game two on time after Erdogmus found a defensive queen sacrifice. In game three, with three seconds against 30, Erdogmus had no time to notice Nakamura's back rank was as weak as his own.

Erdogmus won game four after a ferocious attack, and that began an odd pattern: his first three wins each came just before a break. After the break Nakamura won game five from a piece-down endgame, converting three pawns against a bishop.

Next he won from a rook down. Erdogmus missed that after [43...Qf4+](#ply-86) it was time to repeat moves.

Erdogmus took the last game of the segment from a lost position. Nakamura had a winning attack with a queen and two knights, hung one of them with little time left, and laughed as he resigned.

**The 3+1 and the bullet**

The 3+1 was Nakamura's best segment. The second consecutive win, the Game of the Day, was a strategic Queen's Gambit Declined that he won as Black.

Another Queen's Gambit Declined came right after the break. Nakamura skipped a queen promotion that would have won a rook and played the unusual 43...Ng1 checkmate. He entered the bullet segment six points ahead.

In the bullet, two long draws drained nearly half of the match clock. Erdogmus then won two straight games, the first after a touched f-pawn led to trouble on the g1-a7 diagonal.

In the second, Nakamura called a bluff on the e-file and won a free piece. Erdogmus took the final game, which mattered only for the prize split.

**Money and what's next**

Prize money is divided by win percentage: Nakamura earned $6,500 and Erdogmus $1,625. The tournament carries a $250,000 prize fund and ends with Live Finals in London on February 7, 2027.

Nakamura moves to the quarterfinals against the winner of GM Nihal Sarin vs. GM Hans Niemann. He said he would "much rather play Hans," adding that he wasn't calling Niemann a bad player, and pointed to Nihal's strength in this format.

He already leads the Champions Chess Tour leaderboard, which decides 12 players for the 2027 Esports World Cup. He and Magnus Carlsen have five SCC titles each, as [our earlier piece on the tied record](/articles/speed-chess-championship-xi-carlsen-and-nakamura-tied-at-five-titles/) noted, and either could become the first to win six.

His dark-horse pick for the title was GM Sina Movahed, who plays Carlsen on December 16. Nakamura said Movahed probably should have beaten Lazavik in last year's Esports World Cup. The next Round of 16 match, GM Denis Lazavik against GM Dmitry Andreikin, is Friday, November 20, at 12:00 p.m. ET.

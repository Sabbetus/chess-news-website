Eighteen. That's how many 3+0 Thursday titles IM Renato Terry has now collected after his 9.5/11 performance in the day's final event, a number that starts to look less like a hot streak and more like a monopoly.

Chess.com's speed-chess arenas have produced dominant stretches before, most famously Hikaru Nakamura's run through Titled Tuesday, where for years his name at the top of the crosstable felt like the default outcome rather than a surprise. What made that dominance notable wasn't just the win total, it was the gap: Nakamura wasn't just beating strong grandmasters, he was doing it with a margin that made the rest of the field look like they were playing a different sport.

Terry's 3+0 Thursday record is starting to read the same way, except the venue is bullet chess rather than the 3+1 or blitz formats that made Nakamura's name. Bullet rewards a different skill set: pattern recognition under extreme time pressure, premove discipline, and a tolerance for chaos that even strong classical players sometimes lack. An IM racking up 18 titles in this format, ahead of the GMs and super-GMs who also entered, says something about how format-specific mastery has become in online chess.

It's worth noting that on the same Thursday, GMs Denis Lazavik and Parham Maghsoodloo took the top prizes in other events, which is a reminder that Terry's dominance is specific to this particular arena rather than a blanket statement about his strength relative to the world's elite. That's actually the more interesting story: chess.com's arena ecosystem has fragmented into micro-specialties, where a player can be the undisputed king of one time control and format while grandmasters 200 rating points higher win the headline events elsewhere on the same calendar day.

That fragmentation is new to chess in a way that didn't exist even a decade ago. Classical chess produces one kind of dominance, blitz produces another, and now bullet arenas with their own weekly branding are producing specialists whose names mean something only to the people who follow that specific event. Terry's record is a product of that ecosystem: repeatable, trackable, and entirely a creature of the platform that hosts it.

Whether 18 becomes 25 or 30 depends less on Terry's ceiling and more on whether anyone else decides to specialize as hard as he has. So far, nobody has.

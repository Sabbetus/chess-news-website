The 2026 World U7-U17 Championships in Roquetas de Mar, Spain, has reached its rest day, and [with six rounds played and five to go](https://www.fide.com/one-move-can-change-everything-in-the-world-championships-u7-u17/), the leaderboards are a mix of runaway scores and crowded groups. This is the event whose [new fast classical format](/articles/fast-classical-debuts-at-a-721-player-youth-worlds-in-spain/) we covered at the start, and the gaps are now starting to matter.

Three players have a perfect 6/6: Poland's Ignacy Mongay Miland in the Under-7 Open, Kazakhstan's WCM Milana Filippova in the Under-11 Girls, and Egypt's IM Abdalrahman Sameh Mohamed in the Under-13 Open.

**The pairings that could split the field**

The clearest collision is in the Under-13 Open. Sameh meets Kazakhstan's IM Danis Kuandykuly, the rating favourite, who has dropped only one draw so far. Sameh won the Under-12 category at the 2025 FIDE World Cup, so this is a meeting between two players with real credentials.

In the Under-7 Open, Mongay Miland holds a full-point lead over Egypt's Mohab Ahmed Assem, and the two play each other in Round 7. Seven more players sit on 4.5/6. A win would make Mongay Miland a heavy favourite to stay clear, though five rounds is a long way to go for children this young.

**Girls' sections: Kazakhstan everywhere**

In the Under-7 Girls, Margad Bayasgalan of Mongolia, Leila Aldazharova of Kazakhstan and Canada's Echo Heming Fang share the lead on 5/6. Aldazharova has drawn twice, which FIDE notes is rare in a section where decisive games are the norm.

Filippova leads the Under-11 Girls a full point clear of Georgia's Sara Saltkhutsishvili, and she has already beaten her. She won the Under-10 Asian Championship in 2025. In the Under-9 Girls, Kazakhstan's Amaliya Yedresheva has 5.5/6, half a point ahead of New Zealand's Vera Jintian Xu, while top seed Stella Zhu of Finland is among three players on 4.5.

Kazakhstan also supplies the Under-15 Girls co-leader, WFM Aiaru Altynbek, who shares 5/6 with Vietnam's Vu Nguyen Bao Linh and Mongolia's WCM Batkhishigt Enkhnamuun. In the Under-13 Girls, Germany's WCM Lilian Schirmbeck leads on 5/6 with four wins and two draws, half a point ahead of six players.

**Tight at the top in the Open sections**

In the Under-9 Open, Latvia's Akims Jefimovs and Uzbekistan's Sayfuddin Erkinboev share 5.5/6, with Jefimovs ahead on tiebreak. Estonia's Lev Jevglevski has 5/6. [Tiebreaks](/articles/swiss-system-tiebreaks/) could yet matter in a field this tight.

Uzbekistan's Akbar Mustafakulov, the 2026 Asian Champion, leads the Under-11 Open on 5.5/6, half a point ahead of five players. One of them is top seed Amirzhan Anetov of Kazakhstan, a Candidate Master.

The Under-15 Open looks the most crowded. Kazakhstan's FM Aldiyar Zharas leads on 5.5/6, with five titled players half a point back, among them Spain's FM Martin Krauchi Romero.

**Mate of the day**

FIDE picked a Round 3 finish from the Under-9 Girls: Varvara Volkova, as Black against Symbat Otargali, played 28...Rf1+! Then came 29.Rxf1 Qxf1+ 30.Qxf1 Rxf1#, a forced mate in three.

Round 7 follows the rest day, with five rounds still to be played in every section.

On the penultimate day of the group stage at the Total Chess World Championship Tour Pilot 2026 in Budapest, [Magnus Carlsen can no longer win Group C](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-total-chess-world-championship-tour-pilot-day-5). Two draws against Shakhriyar Mamedyarov left him winless in the fast classical games, on seven points, six behind the new leader Anish Giri.

Only the four group winners go into the knockout for first place. Second-placed players play for fifth, third-placed for ninth, and so on.

**Group C: Giri takes over**

Anish Giri is the new leader. He [beat Abhimanyu Mishra](#game-embed) in the day's Game of the Day, with David Howell calling him "a cold-blooded checkmating machine!" The second game was drawn, and Giri admitted he had been lucky: he mixed up his preparation, then blundered 21.Nd3 and felt "strategically busted" before Mishra suddenly offered a draw.

Giri has 13 points, with Ian Nepomniachtchi and Haik Martirosyan on 11, [FIDE's report](https://www.fide.com/giri-takes-group-c-lead-as-so-wins-twice-on-day-4-in-budapest/) shows. Nepomniachtchi beat the previous leader Martirosyan in the second game of the day.

The arithmetic is simple. In the final round Carlsen plays Nepomniachtchi, Giri plays Mamedyarov and Martirosyan plays Mishra.

Two wins would take Carlsen to 13 at most, so he needs Giri to score nothing. But that would mean Mamedyarov, on nine, winning twice and finishing on 15.

Carlsen's first game against Mamedyarov was the clearest sign of his struggles. Mamedyarov sacrificed a pawn speculatively, and Carlsen answered hesitantly, giving up his queen when he could have reached a near-winning position with queens on. In the second game he needed a win with Black, but equalized carefully and the game fizzled out.

**Group B: Caruana nearly uncatchable**

Fabiano Caruana drew twice with second-placed Liem Le and sits four points clear. Even if he loses both games to Vladislav Artemiev on Sunday, Le would need to beat Praggnanandhaa twice, and Caruana also holds the better tiebreak. Le says he mainly needs to avoid losing that match to secure second.

Caruana is rated 2798.6 to Carlsen's 2804, and he admitted a brief thought about the world number-one spot during the game. Even a win would still have left him 0.4 points short. Our earlier look at [Caruana crossing 2800](/articles/caruana-crosses-2800-and-closes-to-7-2-points-of-carlsen/) shows how quickly that gap has closed.

The group's most startling moment came when Praggnanandhaa, in a winning position against Richard Rapport and short of time, blundered into a mate in one. Rapport's 42.Ne6!!? had been played with 12 seconds left and was tricky to meet.

Praggnanandhaa joked afterwards, "I don't think I can play anything worse than that, so it's fine!" Vincent Keymer, meanwhile, finally got his first win, against Artemiev, who was under a minute by move 19.

**Group A: Firouzja steady**

Alireza Firouzja drew twice with Jan-Krzysztof Duda and leads him by two points. Levon Aronian, also drawing twice with Nodirbek Abdusattorov, explained the flurry of draws this way: "The players who are happy with their tournament situation are stabilizing."

Jorden van Foreest and Andrew Hong traded wins. Van Foreest won the Najdorf after Hong neglected a pawn to attack, and he moved up to joint second. Final-round pairings are Firouzja against Aronian, Van Foreest against Duda, and Hong against Abdusattorov.

**Group D: a three-way tie**

Maxime Vachier-Lagrave still tops the group only because of his second place in the blitz tiebreaker played before the fast classical began. Arjun Erigaisi beat him in the second game of the day to catch him. Hans Niemann joined them after beating Bibisara Assaubayeva, who held the second game over 96 moves despite panicking in time trouble.

Wesley So beat Vaishali Rameshbabu twice and is not quite out of it. He also criticized the 45+30 time control on social media, writing that it feels "more like blitz game than classical chess." The final-round pairings are Assaubayeva against Vachier-Lagrave, Arjun against Vaishali, and Niemann against So.

The final group round starts today, Sunday, November 15, at 9 a.m. ET. The event runs through November 20 as a trial for the 2027 event that will crown the first FIDE World Combined Champion.

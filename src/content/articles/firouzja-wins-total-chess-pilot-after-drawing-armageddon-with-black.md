Alireza Firouzja [came back from a set down to win the Total Chess World Championship Tour pilot](https://www.chess.com/news/view/firouzja-wins-2026-total-chess-world-championship-tour-pilot) in Budapest on its tenth and final day, taking $80,000. Anish Giri won Thursday's opening set 11-9, but Firouzja took Friday's 12-8. That forced an armageddon decider, which he drew with Black to take the title.

**How the final turned**

Each knockout day has seven games: four blitz, two rapid and one fast classic. A blitz win is worth one point, a rapid win four, and a fast classic win eight, with draws worth half. Scores reset daily, and one win apiece sends the match to armageddon.

Giri won the first blitz game with White. Firouzja answered with two straight wins, and Giri leveled at 2-2 in game four.

In the third game Giri was close to a draw in a rook endgame but had five seconds left, and 45...Rf2?? lost. In the fourth, Firouzja hung a bishop.

Firouzja then won the first rapid game with Black, a King's Indian where Giri had the edge but let it slip, and a mating attack came long after the queens were off. A draw in the second rapid game gave him an 8-4 lead.

Giri needed a fast classic win to take the title outright. Firouzja held a draw, the four points each made it 12-8, and the match went to armageddon.

**The decider**

Giri had 10 minutes with White, Firouzja seven with Black plus draw odds. Giri sacrificed an isolated pawn for initiative, but it did not work. Firouzja [accepted a draw on move 35](#game-embed) in a winning position, which was enough.

Firouzja was blunt about the format. "This format helps me more than anyone probably," he said, and the only change he wanted was more value for the blitz games. Asked for his favorite moment, he said: "The last moment!"

Giri blamed "a combination of bad luck and some tiredness" after 48 games in ten days. He took a jab at Magnus Carlsen's absences: "I think in this tournament it's an achievement to show up."

**Third place and the 2027 tour**

Fabiano Caruana beat Maxime Vachier-Lagrave in both sets. The second was 10.5-9.5, and the first rapid game decided it, when Caruana ran a passed f-pawn through. Vachier-Lagrave had led 3.5-0.5 after the blitz, having won the first three games.

Caruana had already qualified for 2027 through the Candidates, as had Giri. That passed the third qualifying spot down to Vachier-Lagrave, who finished fourth. He said he was relieved it was over and called the event "maybe a bit too difficult."

**Other matches**

Hans Niemann beat Jan-Krzysztof Duda in an armageddon to take fifth. He said he felt sick, but was glad to still have "the clutch flagging skills," and hinted he is hoping for a wildcard next year. Ian Nepomniachtchi beat Liem Le with a King's Gambit in armageddon for seventh, after a move-nine blunder in the fast classic.

Haik Martirosyan turned a 3-9 deficit against Arjun Erigaisi into 11-9, winning the fast classic, for ninth. Richard Rapport beat Jorden van Foreest in armageddon for 11th. Wesley So drew armageddon with Black to beat Levon Aronian for 13th.

Vincent Keymer took 15th over Shakhriyar Mamedyarov, who sang happy birthday to his son from the confessional booth mid-game. Andrew Hong finished 19th over Bibisara Assaubayeva. Praggnanandhaa Rameshbabu was 21st over Abhimanyu Mishra, and Nodirbek Abdusattorov beat Vaishali Rameshbabu 20-0 on Friday for 23rd.

**Carlsen's exit**

Carlsen [forfeited the fast classic game](/articles/carlsen-forfeits-two-rapid-games-then-wins-and-stays-above-2800/) against Vladislav Artemiev, his fifth forfeit of the event, then won the armageddon with Black for 17th. A draw would have kept him at 2801.8, but a loss would have dropped him under 2800.

He told Norwegian TV 2 that he had wanted to be with his family. "I'd rather be home than be here," he said. He told Total Chess: "I wanted to play the games I wanted to play."

Giri next has the World Rapid and Blitz Championships in Istanbul at the end of December, and said he takes confidence from this event.

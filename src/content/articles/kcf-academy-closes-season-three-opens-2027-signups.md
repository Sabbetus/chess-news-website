The Kasparov Chess Foundation Academy (KCFA) has [wrapped up its third season](https://en.chessbase.com/post/kcf-academy-third-season-highlights) and opened registration for 2027. The online program is aimed at ambitious players worldwide, and the numbers from this year give a sense of how far the project has spread.

**What the season delivered**

KCFA helped organize the Kasparov Chess Festival in Radenci, which drew 650 players from 33 countries. Afterward the Academy ran an international camp for 65 young players, mixing chess training with swimming and other activities.

Campers also visited the Kasparov Grand Tour and met Garry Kasparov for a photo and autograph session. The season's Garry Kasparov Trainers Conference attracted 150 participants.

The Academy's curriculum rests on Botvinnik's four-level system. Alongside it sit Practical Workshops and Star Lessons, where students learn from players such as Kasparov, Topalov, Aronian, Svidler and Caruana.

In 2026 KCFA added a program for national Olympic teams on preferential terms. KCF trainers also coach a selected group of top juniors, who meet for an annual live camp led by Kasparov during the Zagreb Grand Tour. A selection camp in Munich, run by trainer Roman Vidonyak with support from Chess Club Bayern, targeted the next generation.

**Results from KCF participants**

The Academy points to several achievements by people in its programs. Anastasia Hnatyshyn of Ukraine became European Women's Champion, and the Academy congratulated her coaches Volodymyr Grabinsky and Adrian Mikhalchishin. Ihor Samunenkov was European Team Champion with Ukraine in 2025, and Lukas Dotzer won a European Youth medal.

At the 2026 European Youth Rapid Championship, Bozhena Piddubna and Maksym Pankiv, both from Ukraine, took the titles. Maksym Dubnevych and Peter Balint won medals.

The Academy also says IGM Samunenkov won gold at the 2026 Samarkand Olympiad, finishing ahead of World Champion Gukesh. Samarkand was also where Bodhana Sivanandan, at 11, secured her final WGM norm and became the youngest Woman Grandmaster in history. We covered her other milestone in [her record-setting female IM title](/articles/bodhana-sivanandan-11-beats-polgar-s-1988-age-record-for-female-im/).

**The 2027 season**

The main season runs from January 9 to June 23, 2027. It combines level-based training, thematic workshops and masterclasses. Six of those will be with Veselin Topalov, who is promised to cover attacking play, preparation, critical decisions and performing under pressure.

The KCF STARS Masterclass Series adds a session with Kasparov himself, plus lectures from other grandmasters and coaches. Weekly training totals six hours per level. There are four levels plus an Expert level for national teams and strong club players.

Members can also enter the Kasparov Chess Foundation Friendship Festival 2027, an in-person event with tournaments and training. More camps with top trainers are planned.

**Preseason Special**

Before the main season, a Preseason Special offers 10 live lectures of 90 minutes each, every Saturday at 18:00 CET, on Zoom. The source gives the dates as October 17 to December 19, 2027, which is probably a typo for 2026, since the main season starts in January 2027.

The focus is strategic and practical thinking. All materials, including past lessons, Master Classes and Star Lessons, are available in the KCF Academy App. Details and sign-up are at kcfacademy.org.

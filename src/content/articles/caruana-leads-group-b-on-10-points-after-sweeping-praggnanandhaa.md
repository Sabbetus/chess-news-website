At the Total Chess World Championship Tour Pilot 2026 in Budapest, [Fabiano Caruana and Hans Niemann were the only players to score six points](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-total-chess-world-championship-tour-pilot-day-3) on day three, each winning both games of their mini-match.

Caruana beat Praggnanandhaa Rameshbabu twice and now leads Group B with 10 points and a 3077 performance. Niemann beat Vaishali Rameshbabu twice to join Maxime Vachier-Lagrave on eight points atop Group D.

The format is what makes this so lopsided. Each pairing plays two games with colours reversed, and a win is worth three points against one for a draw. A 3-3 split beats two draws, which is why a double win opens a gap that the other results in a group can't easily close.

**Caruana's day**

Game one was a 26-move miniature.

Caruana said he spotted the sacrifice when he played [17.b3](#ply-33): "I was looking at this sacrifice, because he wants to stop some Ng5 ideas... And I didn't expect he would play it because it looked very dangerous for him. I didn't hesitate too much."

In game two he "really wasn't even playing for a win," but Praggnanandhaa collapsed in a slightly worse endgame. Praggnanandhaa is last in the group, eight points behind. He won Norway Chess 2026 with four straight wins, so a late run is not out of character.

Liem Le, two points behind Caruana, won a rook endgame against Vincent Keymer in game one. He could not finish the second, where promoting a second queen turned out to be the mistake.

**Niemann settles a score**

Vaishali had beaten Niemann in round one of the Blitz Tiebreaker, which he finished fifth on 5.5/9. "Everyone saw my first-round loss and they assumed I'm not a good blitz player anymore," he said. He added: "We live in a short-form society, so that's okay."

He was the first player to win twice in a day. In game two he found a precise way to force his a-pawn through to promotion, a reminder of how badly a knight fares against a rook pawn. He is back over 2700 on the live rating list.

**Assaubayeva's best win**

Bibisara Assaubayeva, rated 2532, beat the 2746-rated Arjun Erigaisi with Black in a King's Indian Defense and called it her best game of the year. "Before the tournament I was scared to score zero out of everything," she said.

Her previous best scalp was Sanan Sjugirov, rated 2691. Arjun won the second game, and both sit on five points, three behind the Group D leaders.

Vachier-Lagrave beat Wesley So in game one when So missed 56...Qe1+, the last chance to hold a queen endgame, and drew game two. So said he found his "worst chess format" and should stay away from it.

**Elsewhere**

In Group A, Alireza Firouzja leads on eight points after beating Jorden van Foreest and drawing. Van Foreest missed 62...Kd4!, which would have won back the b3-pawn in a game he had nearly saved.

Jan-Krzysztof Duda won a 92-move game against Nodirbek Abdusattorov but lost the second, missing 17.Bxh6! with almost 19 minutes on the clock. He is a point behind.

In Group C, Ian Nepomniachtchi drew twice with Shakhriyar Mamedyarov and shares the lead on six points with Haik Martirosyan.

Martirosyan lost game one to Anish Giri, who won in 26 moves and then entered the confessional for about six seconds to say, "I think it's checkmate." Martirosyan won game two in a time scramble, and Giri said, "I am super pissed."

Magnus Carlsen is still winless after four draws. He was down two pawns against Abhimanyu Mishra in game two before holding an opposite-color bishop endgame. Mishra said, "not winning hurts a lot."

Only four of the 10 group games have been played. Rounds five and six come on Friday, November 13, at 9 a.m. ET, and Carlsen will meet Martirosyan for the first time in classical.

Javokhir Sindarov's route to the World Championship match against Gukesh in Geneva is unusual for the contenders of his generation. He was a prodigy who stalled for years before something clicked.

The numbers explain why he is favourite. Over the twelve months before the match, Sindarov's average performance rating is 2812, higher than anyone else's. He has gained 61 Elo points in that time, while Gukesh has lost 53, even though Sindarov is only fourth in the world rankings.

**An early start, then a slow middle**

Sindarov was born on 8 December 2005 in Tashkent and learned chess at four and a half from his grandfather Komil Sindarov, a crime writer and vice-president of the Uzbek Chess Federation. At six he won the Asian U7 School Championship.

In October 2018, aged twelve years, ten months and eight days, he completed his final grandmaster norm. That made him the second-youngest grandmaster in history at the time, five days faster than Praggnanandhaa's record from four months earlier. Early coaches included FM Sirojiddin Zaynidinov and later GM Marat Dzhumaev.

Then the pace dropped. Gukesh, Praggnanandhaa, Abdusattorov and Firouzja shot into the elite, while Sindarov crossed 2600 only in late 2021 and reached 2700 two years after that. Perhaps he lacked his contemporaries' dedication to training; he is said to have played video games secretly at night even at six.

**Quarantine, then Vidonyak**

Sindarov says the Covid lockdowns changed him: "Quarantine changed my life." He trained harder and joined the young Uzbek team that won gold at the 2022 Olympiad in Chennai.

The real jump came after he met Ukrainian IM Roman Vidonyak, a Munich-based coach, in January 2025. Vidonyak built a systematic programme with thousands of exercises, often eight hours a day, mostly without engines and heavy on calculation. They spent 140 to 160 days together at camps.

Sindarov's own account is blunt. Training used to bore him, and he would drift to computer games. With Vidonyak, he says, "time passes so quickly that sometimes we work for eight hours without even noticing."

**World Cup, Candidates, Clutch**

In November 2025 Sindarov won the World Cup in Goa, the youngest winner in its history, which qualified him for the [Candidates Tournament](/events/candidates-tournament/#how-it-works) in Cyprus. He started as sixth seed and led after four rounds on 3½/4, having beaten Caruana. He then beat Nakamura and Wei Yi, drew Giri, and reached the halfway point on 6/7.

He won with 10/14 (+6, =8, -0), a performance rating above 2900, clinching first a round early. No one has done better since the current eight-player, fourteen-round format began.

His round-eleven draw against Caruana, in which he defended for much of the game, is the one he called "perhaps one of the best defensive performances in history." Vidonyak rated it above the six wins. Sindarov also said of the event: "I felt I could see the best moves for both sides."

In late October he won the Clutch Chess event in Saint Louis ahead of Praggnanandhaa, Caruana and Nakamura. Of his Black game against Nakamura he said it was "one of the best King's Indian games I've ever played."

**What to expect in Geneva**

Vidonyak once called his cheerful pupil "Sunny Boy" and now calls him "TNT". Sindarov still plays Counter-Strike; during the Candidates he spent about an hour and a half at his computer most evenings.

The two are six months apart in age and have played few classical games, with Sindarov leading two wins to one draw.

He is respectful: "I am preparing for the best version of Gukesh."

He also said, "This will be the toughest match of my life. I don't know if I'll get a second chance."

For context, [Kasparov leans toward Gukesh, while Nakamura and Carlsen pick Sindarov](/articles/kasparov-leans-gukesh-nakamura-and-carlsen-pick-sindarov/).

The match is 14 classical games at 15.00 CET at the Fondation Martin Bodmer in Cologny. The opening ceremony is on 22 November and game one on 24 November. It ends early if a player reaches 7½ points, and tiebreaks, if needed, are on 12 December.

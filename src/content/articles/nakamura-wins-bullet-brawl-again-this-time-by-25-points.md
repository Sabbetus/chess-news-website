GM Hikaru Nakamura [won Bullet Brawl for the third consecutive week on September 26](https://www.chess.com/news/view/hikaru-nakamura-wins-bullet-brawl-09-26-2026), finishing with 172 points. That was a 25-point margin over the rest of the field.

GM Tuan Minh Le took second place with 147 points. He finished three points ahead of GM Oleksa..., whose name and total are cut off in the excerpt we had, so we can't say more about who completed the podium or what they scored.

The margin is the main thing here. A 25-point gap is not a photo finish. Second place was 147, and Nakamura's 172 sat well above it, so the outcome was settled by a comfortable distance rather than by a late swing.

Le's second place is a decent result in its own right. He held off the next player by only three points, so the fight for the runner-up spot was much closer than the fight for the win.

For context, The Chess Herald recently looked at [Nakamura's 66th Bullet Brawl win](/articles/nakamura-s-66th-bullet-brawl-win-extends-an-absurd-streak/). This latest victory continues that run, and the three-week streak is only the most recent stretch of a much longer record of wins in the event.

Three in a row also means the rest of the field has had three separate chances to close the gap and hasn't. Bullet is the format where Nakamura's speed and premove habits are most visible, and a 25-point cushion suggests that even a strong field of grandmasters is playing for second.

Bullet Brawl runs weekly, so the next test comes next week. A fourth straight title would extend the streak, and the question for the challengers is whether anyone can get within 25 points before then.

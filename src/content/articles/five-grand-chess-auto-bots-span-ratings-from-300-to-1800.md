Chess.com has [launched the Grand Chess Auto Bots for November](https://www.chess.com/news/view/challenge-the-grand-chess-auto-bots), a five-character set of themed opponents. Beat them before the end of the month and you earn exclusive rewards. The site does not say here what those rewards are.

The bots are styled as a crew of crooks who "traded getaway cars for chess pieces." The playing strengths are given as 300, 650, 1000, 1300 and 1800, so the ladder is easy to read.

**The lineup**

Marko sits at the bottom at 300. He is described as a tough-looking guy with a soft spot for his mom, and the pitch is that you can give him "a little chess therapy." For a brand-new player, that is a first win waiting to happen.

Jamie is next at 650. He calls himself Florida's finest criminal mastermind, though the police reports call him a regular. His schemes, we are told, have a habit of falling apart.

Luna plays at 1000. She is billed as a master of deception, and the warning is to keep a close eye on your king. By the time you figure out her plan, "you'll probably be in check."

Troy is rated 1300, with a brilliant mind, a criminal streak and "a few loose screws." He keeps everyone guessing.

TJ tops the set at 1800. He protects his turf and does not take kindly to uninvited guests. Get careless, the description says, and he will show you the door.

**Reading the ladder**

The spacing is deliberate. The first steps climb by 350, 350 and 300 points, then 500 at the top. TJ is the biggest single jump, which suggests he is the real test for the casual club player.

Ratings on Chess.com's bots are not the same scale as FIDE ratings, and the source does not claim they are.

Treat the numbers as a relative guide: a player who beats Troy comfortably but struggles with TJ has found their level on this site.

For a deeper look at what a number like this does and does not predict, see our explainer on [what a chess rating measures](/articles/chess-ratings/).

**What it is for**

Seasonal bot sets like this give newer players a reason to log in and finish a short list of opponents, with each personality offering a small story. Players at 300 or 650 get a realistic target, while the 1800 bot gives stronger players something to chase.

Chess.com also invites readers to report their "chess crimes" in the comments. The month-long window means there is no rush, but the rewards disappear when November ends.

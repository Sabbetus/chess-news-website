Alireza Firouzja and Anish Giri [won their semifinals on Wednesday](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-total-chess-world-championship-tour-pilot-day-8) and will meet in the Knockout 1 Final of the Total Chess World Championship Tour Pilot 2026 in Budapest. Giri swept Maxime Vachier-Lagrave in two sets. Firouzja needed an armageddon game to get past Fabiano Caruana.

Each day has seven games: four blitz (3+2), two rapid (15+10) and one fast classical (45+30). A blitz win is worth one point, a rapid win four and a fast classical win eight, with half for a draw.

The scores reset overnight, and winning a day earns a set point. Two set points take the match, and a 1-1 split goes to armageddon.

**Giri stays unbeaten**

Giri had taken Tuesday 14.5-5.5. On Wednesday he won the third blitz game with Black in a Reti.

He neutralized 7.Ne5, reached an equal endgame and doubled his opponent's pawns. Vachier-Lagrave seems to have underestimated 21...Bxc3!, after which the engine calls White strategically lost.

He then won the second rapid game with White against a Queen's Indian pawn sacrifice that gave him the best version of the idea. White won the pawn back quickly.

Giri drew the fast classical game from a better position and called that "a sin", but it sealed the day 12.5-7.5. He finished unbeaten across all fourteen games.

Giri was candid about the opposition: "Maxime is obviously a much better player in this type of format." On the Final he said, "Maybe I'll get lucky!"

**Firouzja survives Caruana's comeback**

Firouzja had won Tuesday 12-8 and started Wednesday by winning three of four blitz games. Caruana took the fourth.

A rapid win for Caruana, worth four points, wiped out that blitz lead. Firouzja then needed to win the fast classical game with Black.

Instead he played the unusual 2...Qa5 against the Alapin Sicilian, self-destructed and lost. Caruana took the day 15-5.

In the second rapid game he had also found the winning counterblow 38...Rc2! in a time scramble, with Firouzja down to 16 seconds. The loss left Caruana at 2794, seven points behind Magnus Carlsen on the live list, a gap he can still close in his remaining fast classical games.

Caruana's higher two-day total gave him the choice of colour in armageddon, and he took Black. Firouzja, with White, won a pawn early in an Italian Opening.

Caruana lost his a-pawn and the position collapsed. David Howell's verdict on the broadcast was "The black position is cooked." [Caruana ran out of time after Firouzja's 44th move](#game-embed).

Afterward Firouzja said the format's blitz value is the one thing he would change. "On the blitz point system it's really not fair that I won three games and after I lose one game in rapid you're down in the match," he said.

**Elsewhere in the brackets**

In Knockout 2, Hans Niemann swept Liem Le, and Jan-Krzysztof Duda beat Ian Nepomniachtchi in armageddon after losing Tuesday's set. Duda called the win "a lucky game" and said he would have lost had the increment started after move 60 rather than move 41.

Arjun Erigaisi beat Jorden van Foreest in armageddon and meets Haik Martirosyan. Levon Aronian and Wesley So set up an all-American Final. Magnus Carlsen faces Vladislav Artemiev, and Praggnanandhaa Rameshbabu plays Abhimanyu Mishra.

Carlsen, Richard Rapport and Mishra skipped their fast classical games after their matches were decided, extending [the forfeit row from Tuesday](/articles/firouzja-and-giri-take-semifinal-leads-at-total-chess/). A draw would have dropped Carlsen under 2800 for the first time in 17 years.

Mishra, answering criticism from FIDE Enterprises CEO Emil Sutovsky, cited fatigue, the chief arbiter's permission and uncertainty over whether the games would be rated.

Giri and Firouzja play Thursday and Friday, with any armageddon on Friday. Caruana and Vachier-Lagrave meet for third place.

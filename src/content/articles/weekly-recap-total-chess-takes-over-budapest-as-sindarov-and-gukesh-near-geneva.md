Twenty stories this week, and the biggest share came out of Budapest, where the Total Chess pilot opened and quickly rearranged the top of the rating list. Around it sat a milestone for Indian chess, two anniversaries, and a look ahead to the World Championship in Geneva.

**Total Chess in Budapest**

The [Total Chess pilot brought 24 players to Budapest](/articles/total-chess-budapest-pilot-how-the-format-works/), Keymer among them, for a mix of blitz, rapid and 45-minute "Fast Classic" games.

Magnus Carlsen opened the event by [winning the nine-round blitz Swiss on 7/9](/articles/carlsen-wins-the-total-chess-blitz-swiss-on-7-9-unbeaten/), unbeaten. Maxime Vachier-Lagrave took second on 6.5 and Levon Aronian third on 6.

The Fast Classic group stage began with [six leaders on four points](/articles/football-scoring-leaves-carlsen-and-giri-behind-after-two-draws/). The more interesting numbers sat lower down, where football-style scoring left Carlsen and Giri behind after two draws.

On day three, [Caruana and Niemann were the only players to reach six points](/articles/caruana-leads-group-b-on-10-points-after-sweeping-praggnanandhaa/), each winning both games of their mini-match. Caruana now leads Group B on 10 after sweeping Praggnanandhaa.

That form carried into the ratings. [Caruana reached exactly 2800.0 on the live list](/articles/caruana-crosses-2800-and-closes-to-7-2-points-of-carlsen/) on day four, 7.2 points behind Carlsen, who began the event 40 points clear.

By the end of day five [Giri led Group C and Carlsen, still winless in Fast Classic, could no longer win it](/articles/carlsen-eliminated-in-group-c-as-caruana-nears-group-b-win/), while Caruana sat four points clear in Group B.

Off the board, FIDE President Timur Turlov [held a string of meetings in Budapest](/articles/turlov-meets-total-chess-and-norway-chess-backers-in-budapest/) after the opening ceremony. FIDE says the talks covered international cooperation and new areas of its work.

**Geneva and Wijk aan Zee**

The World Championship in Geneva starts in just over a week. In a Chess.com roundup, [most strong players give Sindarov the edge](/articles/kasparov-leans-gukesh-nakamura-and-carlsen-pick-sindarov/), though few predict a landslide. Kasparov leans toward Gukesh, while Nakamura and Carlsen back Sindarov.

Sindarov also features in the [Tata Steel 2027 field announcement](/articles/tata-steel-2027-abdusattorov-and-sindarov-lead-a-young-field/). He and defending champion Abdusattorov head the list for the 89th Wijk aan Zee, which runs January 15 to 31.

**Indian successes and open events**

Anuj Shrivatri, 22, [became India's 100th grandmaster](/articles/anuj-shrivatri-becomes-india-s-100th-grandmaster-in-the-philippines/) by winning the ASEAN+ Open in the Philippines on 7/9. He finished alone in first despite losing his opening round.

Pranav V, already World Junior Champion in classical, [added the blitz title in Al Ain](/articles/pranav-v-adds-the-world-junior-blitz-title-with-10-points/) with 10½ points, a full point clear.

In Germany, Krishnan Sasikiran [won the Bavarian Open on tiebreaks](/articles/sasikiran-wins-bavarian-open-on-tiebreak-after-four-share-7/) with 7½/9, in a field of 548 players. Svane took second.

**Championships**

In Serbia, Galina Strutinskaya, [perfect on 5/5 at the start of the week](/articles/strutinskaya-is-5-5-at-senior-worlds-while-open-sections-bunch-up/), [won the Women 65+ at the World Senior Championships on 10/11](/articles/strutinskaya-wins-women-65-on-10-11-at-senior-worlds/). Pia Cramling took the Women 50+.

In Shaoxing, Yu Yangyi [kept his Chinese Rapid title](/articles/yu-yangyi-wins-armageddon-to-keep-chinese-rapid-title/). He beat 18-year-old Xiao Tong 4-3 in an Armageddon decider after six games failed to separate them.

Online, Dmitry Andreikin [won two of the three 3 0 Thursday events](/articles/andreikin-wins-two-3-0-thursdays-xiong-stops-the-sweep/) and finished second in the third, just behind Jeffery Xiong. Nobody has ever won more than two in a week.

**Remembering two greats**

Mikhail Tal would have turned 90 on Monday. [Our portrait of the Riga genius](/articles/mikhail-tal-at-90-the-genius-who-gave-away-everything/) covers the man who carried no wallet, won six Soviet titles and went 86 games unbeaten.

Twenty-five years ago, on 12 November 2001, [Tony Miles was found dead in his bed](/articles/tony-miles-england-s-first-grandmaster-dead-25-years/). England's first grandmaster died of heart failure linked to long-term diabetes, after an old school friend came to collect him for bridge.

**Tournament calendars**

The largest field on our October list for Africa was the [Open 1er Novembre in Medea](/articles/medea-open-draws-201-as-africa-s-classical-calendar-fills-up/), Algeria. It registered 201 players for a classical event from October 29 to November 1.

In Oceania, the [Darling Downs Open Premier in Toowoomba](/articles/darling-downs-open-opens-oceania-s-october-with-two-sections/) headed our October list. It ran five days, from 1 to 5 October.

Looking to December, Europe has 284 tournaments tracked, and we picked out [the Rilton Cup and the Skopje Balkan Championship](/articles/rilton-cup-and-skopje-balkan-championship-top-europe-s-december/) among the ones worth circling.

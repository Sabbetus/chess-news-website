Samarkand carried most of this week's weight, hosting both the Disability Olympiad and the record-breaking 46th Chess Olympiad back to back. Around that, FIDE lined up a new commercial partner for the World Championship, and the usual scattering of upsets and calendar data rounded things out.

[Freedom Holding Corp. signed on as General Partner of the 2026 World Championship Match](/articles/freedom-holding-signs-on-as-fide-s-newest-world-title-backer/), putting a NASDAQ-listed financial and tech group's name behind chess's biggest fixture.

The match itself now has a home. [The Salesforce FIDE World Championship Match 2026 will run November 22 to December 12 at the Fondation Martin Bodmer in Geneva](/articles/geneva-s-return-to-world-title-chess-22-years-on/), pitting reigning champion Gukesh D against challenger Javokhir Sindarov, the city's first world title match in 22 years.

Hikaru Nakamura kept stretching a streak that's stopped looking like news and started looking like a habit. [He won Bullet Brawl for a record 66th time on September 12](/articles/nakamura-s-66th-bullet-brawl-win-extends-an-absurd-streak/), a margin that felt routine for him and staggering for everyone else.

Samarkand's Disability Olympiad kept building on its opening round. [Round 2 pushed the field further, with eight teams reaching a perfect four match points](/articles/samarkand-s-disability-olympiad-hits-a-record-40-teams/) in what organizers called a record 40-team turnout.

By Round 3, only [Cuba, Israel and the Czech Republic remained on 6/6](/articles/samarkand-s-deaf-champion-coached-its-future-star/), with Round 4 pitting Cuba against Israel directly and the Czechs facing Germany. That round produced its result quickly: [Cuba beat Israel 2½-1½ to stay perfect through four matches](/articles/cuba-stays-perfect-as-the-disability-olympiad-nearly-doubles/), stretching its run to eight match points.

Cuba's run didn't survive Round 5. [Round 5 handed Cuba its first defeat of the tournament](/articles/cuba-s-unbeaten-run-ends-as-poland-and-czechia-share-the-lead/), reshaping the standings into a share of the lead between Poland and Czechia just as the main Olympiad opened in the same city.

Poland didn't stay tied for long. Heading into the penultimate round, [Poland pulled clear of the Czech Republic with a 4-0 rout](/articles/poland-s-4-0-rout-puts-it-alone-atop-samarkand/) to sit alone at the top.

That lead held to the finish. [Poland went from a Round 1 loss to an undefeated title run](/articles/poland-s-rollercoaster-ends-in-gold-at-samarkand/), taking gold on 13 match points from a record 41-team field, with Cuba claiming silver on 12 and hosts Uzbekistan 1 taking bronze on 11.

While that was wrapping up, the main event was setting its own record. [The 46th FIDE Chess Olympiad drew 208 teams in the Open and 192 in the Women's section](/articles/samarkand-s-400-teams-break-budapest-s-record-by-20/), a combined 400 that beat Budapest's 2024 mark of 380.

The opening press conference wasn't quite the victory lap it usually is. Five months into his job as FIDE Interim President, [Viswanathan Anand spent part of his time at the microphone doing damage control](/articles/anand-admits-the-cracks-behind-samarkand-s-record-olympiad/) instead of taking one.

Round 1 played out the way a mismatched Swiss field usually does, with favorites rolling and a handful of shocks. [India's bench held even as Arjun Erigaisi fell on the top board](/articles/erigaisi-falls-but-india-s-bench-holds-at-samarkand/) in Samarkand.

Round 2 delivered the tournament's biggest surprise so far. [El Salvador, seeded 94th, beat 44th-seeded Cuba](/articles/el-salvador-s-94th-seed-stuns-cuba-at-samarkand/), a result with little connection to the pecking order on paper.

The same round wasn't kind to some other favorites either. [FIDE's own recap flagged upsets and near-disasters across the day](/articles/liang-s-shock-loss-and-montenegro-s-draw-rattle-samarkand/), on a day that was supposed to be a settling-in round for the top seeds.

Round 3 hit the Women's section hardest. [28th-seeded Argentina knocked out Ukraine, and Iran beat eighth-seeded France 3-1](/articles/argentina-s-28th-seed-shocks-ukraine-at-samarkand/), results FIDE itself called the biggest surprises of the day.

By Round 4, the field of unbeaten teams had thinned. [China, Uzbekistan, Germany, Armenia, the USA, India, the Netherlands and Hungary all stayed perfect in the Open section](/articles/eight-teams-still-perfect-after-four-rounds-in-samarkand/), while India, Kazakhstan, China and the USA led a larger unbeaten group in the Women's event.

Away from Samarkand, the tournament-calendar data kept tracking where the volume actually sits. Europe's October slate runs to 332 events, topped by [the 29. Offene Internationale Bayerische Schachmeister in Gmund am Tegernsee with 340 players](/articles/bavaria-s-340-player-open-headlines-a-packed-october-in-europe/), while Asia's 181 tracked tournaments are [led once again by India's grassroots rush of national opens, school championships and rapid weekenders](/articles/india-s-october-grassroots-rush-fills-asia-s-calendar/).

North America and South America told a similar story. [Toronto's Junior Championship tops a North American slate of 109 tracked events](/articles/toronto-s-junior-championship-tops-a-crowded-october-slate/), a field skewed by patchy US reporting, while in South America [Quito's II Escolar Internacional Atahualpa draws 174 players](/articles/ecuador-s-atahualpa-twins-top-a-114-event-october/) to top a 114-event October.

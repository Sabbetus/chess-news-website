Hikaru Nakamura has [signed a new long-term agreement with Chess.com](https://www.chess.com/news/view/hikaru-nakamura-renews-with-chess-com), extending a partnership that began in 2013. The 38-year-old world number two is both a top-level player and one of the game's most influential content creators.

The deal has near-term and longer-term parts. He will join the Chess.com commentary team for some key games at the Salesforce FIDE World Championship 2026 and cover every day of the event on his Kick channel. A Hikaru vs. The World match is planned for 2027.

**What he says about the ride**

Nakamura looked back at how much has changed. "It's been an incredible journey seeing Chess.com grow from a small company with no more than 50 employees to what it is today," he said.

He named his turning points: Danny Rensch, then "a small-time executive," reaching out to convince him to stream, and covering the 2018 World Championship with its monkey-clapping memes. Then came the 2020 and 2021 boom, with xQc, Reckful and other streamers playing chess, ending in PogChamps and shows like The Queen's Gambit.

Rensch, now Chess.com's Chief Chess Officer, said Nakamura has been there "from every edition of the Speed Chess Championship to the PRO Chess League, and PogChamps, to countless Titled Tuesdays and Bullet Brawls." He added that Nakamura "has inspired generations and blazed a trail for those who will come after him."

**The streaming numbers**

Chess.com's announcement lists his stream records. His peak audience was 84,403 viewers, during the Magnus Carlsen Invitational on March 16, 2021. His best day for new subscribers brought 13,609, on January 8, 2021.

He won 52 bullet games in a single hour on June 1, 2021. His Human Benchmark Chimp Test best is 30, and he once ate five bananas on stream, in January 2021.

His Bullet Brawl peaks are a 320-point score and a 59-game streak. His social following totals 8.6 million.

The PogChamps moment Chess.com singles out is his commentary with WFM Alexandra Botez of the xQc vs. Moistcr1tikal match, which ended much sooner than either expected. That clip has more than 26 million views.

**The board record**

The renewal also leans on his over-the-board career, which is unusual for a streamer. He has five U.S. Championship titles, the 2011 Tata Steel Chess Masters, the 2022 Fischer Random World Championship and the 2023 Norway Chess title.

He also played the 2025 Louisiana State Championship, which paved his road to this year's Candidates, his fourth. He posted YouTube recaps of his games from that event, a habit that sums up the dual career the deal is built around. For readers new to the format, [the Candidates](/events/candidates-tournament/#how-it-works) decides who challenges for the world title.

On Chess.com itself he won five straight Speed Chess Championships from 2018 to 2022. He has four Bullet Chess Championship titles, including a 17-15 final win over Magnus Carlsen in 2023. That puts him level with Carlsen, as [our look at Speed Chess Championship XI](/articles/speed-chess-championship-xi-carlsen-and-nakamura-tied-at-five-titles/) noted.

His blitz rating on the site now stands at a record 3503. He leads Titled Tuesday with 94 wins in the 11-round era, and holds 73 Bullet Brawl titles, more than twice the next-highest total.

**What to watch**

The commentary booth is the first test of the new chapter. The Hikaru vs. The World match, set for 2027, is the headline item for fans, though the announcement gives no format or date yet.

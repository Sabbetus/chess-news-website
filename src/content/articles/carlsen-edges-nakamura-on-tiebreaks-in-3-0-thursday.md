They aren't playing in the ongoing Chess Olympiad in Samarkand, but that didn't stop GM Magnus Carlsen and GM Hikaru Nakamura from showing up for [the debut edition of 3 0 Thursday on September 24](https://www.chess.com/news/view/carlsen-maghsoodloo-ashraf-3-0-thursday-09-24-2026), Chess.com's new weekly bullet event. Both players finished tied atop the standings, and it was Carlsen who came out on top once tiebreaks were applied.

What makes the result notable is how it happened. Carlsen's edge came with an assist from a mouse slip, the kind of accidental click that can decide bullet games as easily as any real blunder. It's a reminder of how thin the margins get once the clock drops to three minutes with no increment, where a single miscue with the mouse can matter as much as the moves themselves.

Nakamura's presence at the top of the standings fits a pattern the site has tracked closely. [Nakamura has built an absurd streak of Bullet Brawl wins](/articles/nakamura-s-66th-bullet-brawl-win-extends-an-absurd-streak/), and a tie for first in a brand-new bullet format on his very first appearance shows that dominance in fast time controls translating across formats, not just the one event where he's made his name.

Away from the top of the table, GM Parham Maghsoodloo and GM Ashraf both picked up victories on the day. Their wins didn't carry the same tiebreak drama as the Carlsen-Nakamura finish, but they add to the day's story of established grandmasters asserting themselves in a first-time event still finding its footing.

The heartbreak belonged to Minh Le, who came up just short on the day. Bullet events like this one are unforgiving in exactly that way: a player can do everything right for most of an event and still end up on the wrong side of the standings once the fastest games get decided by inches rather than strategy.

With the Olympiad still dominating the sport's attention out of Samarkand, it says something that Carlsen and Nakamura chose to spend their Thursday in a brand-new bullet format instead of sitting it out. Whether 3 0 Thursday becomes a fixture on their calendars the way Bullet Brawl has for Nakamura is the open question heading into next week's edition.

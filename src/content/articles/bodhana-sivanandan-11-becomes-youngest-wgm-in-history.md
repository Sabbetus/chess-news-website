England put its youngest player on top board at the Women's Olympiad in Samarkand, and Bodhana Sivanandan, aged 11, [finished with 8/11 and the third norm she needed to become a Woman Grandmaster](https://en.chessbase.com/post/bodhana-sivanandan-youngest-ever-wgm-at-11). That makes her the youngest WGM in chess history.

The selection was less of a gamble than it sounds. Her rating of 2354 before the event made her England's number one woman, well ahead of the experienced Jovanka Houska and Harriet Hunt.

**The record**

Kateryna Lagno received the title in 2002 at twelve. Hou Yifan, later Women's World Champion, also got it at twelve, in 2007. Sivanandan has now passed both. FIDE introduced the WGM title in 1976.

Those two names are a useful yardstick. Lagno became a top-level grandmaster and Hou Yifan dominated women's chess for years, so the age bracket Sivanandan has just entered has a strong track record.

**The tournament**

She played all eleven rounds and opened with three wins. In round four she drew with Azerbaijan's IM Ulviyya Fataliyeva (2402).

Her draw with Anastasiia Hnatyshyn (2463) stands out. Hnatyshyn is the world's top junior and reigning European Women's Champion. Sivanandan, playing Black, held her.

She also [drew with Georgia's GM Nana Dzagnidze](#game-embed), rated 2478, the highest-rated opponent on her card. Dzagnidze is an experienced Olympiad regular, and she could not get past an 11-year-old.

**The full card**

Her opponents in order: WFM Hannah Wilson (BAR, 1835), win; WFM Deborah Quickpen (NGR, 2174), win; FM Juliana Terao (BRA, 2200), win; Fataliyeva, draw; WFM Shen Zhiyuan (AUS, 2146), win; Hnatyshyn, draw; WFM Lala Shohradova (TKM, 2147), win.

Then came IM Deysi Cori (PER, 2316), the only loss, in round eight. After that, IM Mai Narva (EST, 2411) was a draw, Dzagnidze a draw, and WGM Janelle Mae Frayna (PHI, 2260) a win to close.

Her performance rating was 2432, above her own 2354 rating. Only one of the eleven games was lost.

**Team impact**

She finished 13th among the players on board one. She was England's best performer and led the team to 15th place, five places above its starting rank based on average rating.

**Where she came from**

Her start in chess was accidental. A visitor left a chessboard at her parents' home at the start of the pandemic, her father taught her the rules, and she improved with YouTube tutorials.

She played her first tournament at Harrow Chess Club in 2021. In 2022, aged seven, she won all 24 of her games at the European Schools Chess Championship. In the summer of 2026 she became the youngest British national champion of all time.

Her next challenge is the jump from title to the open circuit, where rating gains get harder. After a 2432 performance against this field, she goes there with the numbers behind her.

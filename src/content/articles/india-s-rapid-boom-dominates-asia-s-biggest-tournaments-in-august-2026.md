August 2026 was a big month for grassroots chess across Asia, and our database's ranking of tournaments by registered players tells a clear story: India's district- and state-level rapid events, often run out of school halls and community centers, are pulling in crowds that rival major international opens.

Topping the list is the **Festival Catur Nasional Bandar Diraja Kuala Kangsar** in Malaysia, a rapid event that drew 1,194 players, the largest tournament by player count we tracked on the continent this month. Close behind is the **Bengaluru Open FIDE Rated Rapid Chess Tournament**, held at Nexus Mall in Koramangala, with 1,088 players, and the **Dr. S. Varadarajan Memorial Inter School Chess Tournament** in Chennai, which brought in 991 players.

From there, the list is heavily populated by Indian rapid tournaments: Hyderabad's **PICA's All India Open Rapid FIDE Rating Chess Tournament** (864), Navi Mumbai's **1st MindPetal Rising Minds Mumbai Open** (also 864), Bangalore's **Grand Open Rapid FIDE Rated Chess Tournament** at Koramangala Indoor Stadium (727), and more than half a dozen others in the 500-800 range, spanning Chennai, Coimbatore, Karur, Srivilliputhur, Nagercoil, and beyond. Several of these are district- or school-level events, a sign of how much of India's chess growth is happening at the grassroots.

Outside India, notable entries include the **9th Istanbul International Open Chess Tournament** in Türkiye, a classical event running August 24-30 with 904 players, and the **4th Queenstar International Rating Chess Championship** in Gampaha, Sri Lanka, a classical tournament with 899 registered. Vietnam contributed several strong rapid and classical events, including an 816-player youth rapid tournament in Nghệ An and a 613-player classical event in Đà Nẵng. Singapore's **24th ASEAN+ Age-Group Chess Championships**, a classical tournament at Our Tampines Hub, drew 696 players across the region's junior ranks.

None of the tournaments in our data listed a prize pool or rating requirement, and rounds were not recorded for any of them either. These are figures we simply don't have for August, not indications that such events didn't exist.

A caveat worth stressing: this ranking only includes tournaments where a player count was reported to our sources. Asia hosted 1,836 tracked tournaments in August 2026 overall, but the vast majority don't have registration numbers attached, so plenty of significant events, including in countries that don't reliably report player counts, are likely missing from this list entirely. Treat this as a snapshot of the largest *known* fields, not a definitive ranking of the continent's biggest events.

August 2026 was a big month for grassroots chess across Asia. Ranking tournaments by registered players tells a clear story: India's district- and state-level rapid events are pulling in crowds that rival major international opens.

Many of these are run out of school halls and community centers, not dedicated venues.

Topping the list is the [Festival Catur Nasional Bandar Diraja Kuala Kangsar](https://chesstournamentcalendar.com/tournament/festival-catur-nasional-bandar-diraja-kuala-kangsa-1464474/) in Malaysia, a rapid event that drew 1,194 players. That's the largest tournament by player count tracked on the continent this month.

Close behind is the [Bengaluru Open FIDE Rated Rapid Chess Tournament](https://chesstournamentcalendar.com/tournament/bengaluru-open-fide-rated-rapid-chess-tournament-1451286/), held at Nexus Mall in Koramangala, with 1,088 players. The [Dr. S. Varadarajan Memorial Inter School Chess Tournament](https://chesstournamentcalendar.com/tournament/dr-s-varadarajan-memorial-inter-school-chess-tou-1479201/) in Chennai brought in 991.

From there, the list is heavily populated by Indian rapid tournaments: Hyderabad's [PICA's All India Open Rapid FIDE Rating Chess Tournament](https://chesstournamentcalendar.com/tournament/picas-all-india-open-rapid-fide-rating-chess-tour-1444214/) (864), Navi Mumbai's [1st MindPetal Rising Minds Mumbai Open](https://chesstournamentcalendar.com/tournament/1st-mindpetal-rising-minds-mumbai-open-fide-rapid-1383871/) (also 864), and Bangalore's [Grand Open Rapid FIDE Rated Chess Tournament](https://chesstournamentcalendar.com/tournament/grand-open-rapid-fide-rated-chess-tournamentksca-e-1477664/) at Koramangala Indoor Stadium (727).

More than half a dozen others sit in the 500-800 range, spanning Chennai, Coimbatore, Karur, Srivilliputhur, and Nagercoil. Several are district- or school-level events, a sign of how much of India's chess growth is happening at the grassroots.

Outside India, notable entries include the [9th Istanbul International Open Chess Tournament](https://chesstournamentcalendar.com/tournament/9th-istanbul-international-open-chess-tournament-1474885/) in Türkiye, a classical event running August 24-30 with 904 players, and the [4th Queenstar International Rating Chess Championship](https://chesstournamentcalendar.com/tournament/the-4th-queenstar-international-rating-chess-champ-1468435/) in Gampaha, Sri Lanka, with 899 registered.

Vietnam contributed several strong rapid and classical events too. Singapore's [24th ASEAN+ Age-Group Chess Championships](https://chesstournamentcalendar.com/tournament/24th-asean-age-group-chess-championships-2026sta-1424527/), a classical tournament at Our Tampines Hub, drew 696 players across the region's junior ranks.

None of these tournaments listed a prize pool or rating requirement, and rounds weren't recorded for any of them. These are figures that simply aren't available for August, not signs that such events didn't exist.

A caveat worth stressing: this ranking only includes tournaments where a player count was reported. Asia hosted 1,836 tracked tournaments in August 2026 overall, but most don't have registration numbers attached.

Plenty of significant events, including in countries that don't reliably report player counts, are likely missing from this list entirely. Treat this as a snapshot of the largest *known* fields, not a definitive ranking of the continent's biggest events.

The opening day of the Fast Classic group stage at the Total Chess World Championship Tour Pilot 2026 in Budapest [produced six leaders on four points](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-total-chess-world-championship-tour-pilot-day-2), and the most interesting numbers sit lower down the tables.

Football-style scoring, three points for a win and one for a draw, means a win and a loss beats two draws. Magnus Carlsen and Anish Giri found that out the hard way.

**The scoring bites in Group C**

Carlsen and Giri drew both games and each have 2/6. Shakhriyar Mamedyarov and Haik Martirosyan swapped wins with Black and have 3 each, ahead of both of them. Ian Nepomniachtchi leads the group on 4 after a win and a draw against Abhimanyu Mishra.

The draws were far from dull. Carlsen played the Dutch Defense in game one and got the risky structure you would expect.

Giri answered with the pawn sacrifice 16.d5!?, though Chess.com noted 16.dxc5 was perfectly fine, and both players ran low on time before Carlsen traded queens into an endgame. The second game was wilder still, with each man looking like the likelier winner in turn.

Only first place in a group leads to the four-player knockout for the title, so both need to start scoring more heavily. Nepomniachtchi was close to a perfect day: he sacrificed two pawns against Mishra in game one, and 51...b5? was the mistake that cost Mishra. In game two he missed an endgame win and the game was drawn in 98 moves.

**Comebacks in the confessional**

Martirosyan was outplayed by Mamedyarov in game one, but hit back with Black after Mamedyarov was under early pressure, fell behind on the clock and blundered on move 20, resigning the next move. "It's really good that we have so many games in a day, especially after losing," he said.

In Group A, Jorden van Foreest [beat Levon Aronian in game two](#game-embed), which Chess.com named its Game of the Day. He had lost the first game after a "high risk, high reward" opening and going astray on move 15.

"I decided to go all-in!" he said, playing the King's Indian Defense. Aronian lashed out with 21.f5?!, which he called "careless", and Van Foreest said, "I got all the squares." Both finished on 3.

Alireza Firouzja and Jan-Krzysztof Duda lead Group A on 4. Firouzja beat Nodirbek Abdusattorov, who lost a pawn by force with 35...Bf7?, then drew the second game, which lasted 57 moves. Duda drew Andrew Hong, then converted a big space edge into a 10-minute clock lead and won.

**Groups B and D**

Fabiano Caruana and Le Quang Liem lead Group B. Caruana beat Richard Rapport with Black, then could not convert pressure in game two.

Le drew Vladislav Artemiev and then won after Artemiev ended up 40 minutes down on the clock and allowed 22.Nd5!!. Praggnanandhaa Rameshbabu and Vincent Keymer drew both games to share 2 points, according to FIDE's report.

Maxime Vachier-Lagrave leads Group D by two points. He beat Vaishali Rameshbabu in game one, then had an extra pawn and a big time edge in game two but let her draw a rook endgame. Bibisara Assaubayeva held Wesley So twice, and Arjun Erigaisi and Hans Niemann also drew both.

**What comes next**

Day three brings rounds three and four on Thursday, November 12, starting at 9 a.m. ET / 15:00 CET. With 45+30 games played in doubles, the draws that looked like honourable results on Wednesday already read as lost ground. The pilot runs November 10-21 as a trial for the 2027 event that will crown the first FIDE World Combined Champion.

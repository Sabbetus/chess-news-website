After a three-month break, [Freestyle Friday returns to Chess.com on October 2](https://www.chess.com/news/view/freestyle-friday-returns-october-2-with-world-championship-spots-on-the-line), restarting the race for places in the 2027 FIDE Freestyle Chess World Championships. Ten weekly tournaments remain before the December knockouts. The first-half points carry over, so nobody starts from zero.

Play begins at 11 a.m. ET / 5 p.m. CEST / 8:30 p.m. IST, with coverage on Chess.com, the Freestyle Chess YouTube channel and DAZN. Each event is an 11-round Swiss for titled players at 3+2.

**Who is already in**

Five players have qualified for the Open championship: Magnus Carlsen, Fabiano Caruana, Nodirbek Abdusattorov, Vincent Keymer and Pranav Venkatesh. Pranav earned his place by topping the Open standings with 60 points. On the women's side, Anna Muzychuk qualified by leading with 88.

That leaves three of the eight places open in each field. Two further online qualifiers, separate from the Friday series, will offer more routes to Weissenhaus, Germany, where both championships will be held in February.

**How the points race works**

Weekly points run from 10 for first place down to one for eighth through 10th. Players keep adding to their totals every Friday through December 4, with separate Open and Women's leaderboards. The top seven eligible players in each field move on to a knockout stage.

The quarterfinals are on December 11, with the semifinals and final on December 18. The winner in each field takes a World Championship spot. So the standings decide who gets into the bracket, and the bracket decides who gets the berth.

**Why the format is interesting**

A season-long points table rewards consistency over one hot weekend, but a knockout finish keeps the drama alive for anyone who scrapes into the top seven. That hybrid is deliberate. Freestyle Chess CEO Dr. Andreas Walker pitched it with a motorsport comparison: "Formula One has its Grand Prix weekends. Chess now has its Fridays."

Freestyle chess, also called Chess960 or Fischer Random, randomizes the back-rank arrangement, which cuts the value of memorized opening preparation. At 3+2 that should favor quick, instinctive players, though I would not read too much into that before the points start flowing again. Chess960 is also in the news on the governance side, as [FIDE's Assembly backed Chess960 ratings](/articles/fide-assembly-backs-chess960-ratings-and-a-qatar-georgia-2030-pick/).

**Who to watch**

Organizers expect Hans Niemann, Denis Lazavik, Ian Nepomniachtchi and Nodirbek Yakubboev in the first event back. Among the women, Muzychuk and Alexandra Kosteniuk are expected. With the big names already qualified, the open spots are likely to be fought over by the next tier, and the sheer volume of strong titled players makes a top-seven finish far from guaranteed.

The broadcasts also carry a twist: hosts compete while commentating on their own games. The announced lineup is Maxime Vachier-Lagrave, Niemann, Vidit Gujrathi and Oleksandr Bortnyk, plus WFM Anna Cramling, WGM Dina Belenkaya, and from ChessBase India IM Sagar Shah and GM Harshit Raja.

Commentating mid-game is a hard trick, and it may produce some candid moments. Whether it costs the hosts points will be visible in the standings soon enough.

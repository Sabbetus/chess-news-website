FIDE's 3rd Council meeting of 2026, held on September 21, [produced a list of 21 decisions](https://www.fide.com/3rd-fide-council-meeting-2026-list-of-decisions/). Most are routine housekeeping: reports noted, titles approved. A few are worth a closer look, because they show how the federation is handling titles, qualification and the run-up to its General Assembly.

**The youth titles question**

The most substantive item is CM3-2026/07. The Council confirmed that winners of the World Youth Championship U7-U17 2026, held in Spain, will receive titles under the current Direct Titles table.

It then added a second instruction: the Qualification Commission is to prepare general recommendations on whether the Direct Titles table applies to world championships played at a fast classical time control.

That wording suggests the issue is a gap in the rules. The table was written with conventional classical events in mind, and a shortened classical format raises the question of whether the same title reward fits.

The Council has not settled that yet; it has handed the question to the commission. For background on how [classical, rapid and blitz](/articles/time-controls/) differ, and on [how titles are earned](/articles/chess-titles/), our explainers cover the basics.

**Qualification and the World Cup**

CM3-2026/17 approved the ACC distribution of World Cup slots for 2026 for Africa. FIDE also confirmed it will support the formation of the prize fund for the three approved Regional events.

The same item restates a rule: under current regulations, every winner of a qualification tournament that grants a direct World Cup spot is entitled to a Direct Title under the table. Those events feed the [FIDE World Cup](/events/chess-world-cup/#how-it-works), which in turn sits inside [the road to the world title](/articles/world-championship-cycle/).

**Politics and the Assembly**

Under CM3-2026/18 the Council noted a letter from the Ukrainian Chess Federation and instructed the FIDE Office to send a formal answer. The list gives no further detail on the letter's contents or the planned reply.

CM3-2026/19 recommends that the General Assembly support the updated proposal of AIDEF. The Council also approved the final version of the 2026 General Assembly agenda (CM3-2026/21), so the delegates' business is now fixed.

An earlier Chess Herald piece covered [what the Assembly backed on Chess960 ratings and the 2030 host pick](/articles/fide-assembly-backs-chess960-ratings-and-a-qatar-georgia-2030-pick/), a reminder that Council recommendations are only the first step before the Assembly votes.

**Honours and routine business**

CM3-2026/20 recommends that the General Assembly award the Honorary Member title to C. Abundo, G. Makropoulos and E. Torre, and name Bessel Kok a Friend of FIDE.

The rest of the list covers commission work. The Council approved the Arbiters' Commission's recommendations on seminars, classification upgrades, lecturer list amendments, FA norms and titles. It also approved recommendations on titles from the Qualification, Trainers', Events, and Chess in Education commissions, plus the Trainers' Commission's academy decisions and the Treasurer's report.

Reports from the Interim President, the Global Strategy, Arbiters', Qualification, Chess in Education, Social, Technical and Constitutional commissions, and from the Continental Presidents, were simply noted.

The next step is the General Assembly itself, where the recommendations on AIDEF, the Honorary Members and Friend of FIDE will go to a vote.

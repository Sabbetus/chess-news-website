The Global Chess League table has a habit of flipping on its head every couple of days, and Wednesday in Bengaluru was no exception. FYERS American Gambits [added to the misery of Triveni Continental Kings by inflicting yet another defeat on the two-time champions](https://www.fide.com/gcl-2026-day-5-gambits-beat-kings-pipers-defeat-mumba-masters-as-race-to-the-final-heats-up/), beating them 8-5 to reclaim top spot from Alpine APL Pipers, who had briefly led after their own win earlier in the day.

Anyone who followed [GCL's wild Day 3](/articles/gcl-s-wild-day-3-gambits-fall-pipers-seize-the-lead/) will recognize the shape of this. Back then it was the Gambits stumbling and the Pipers grabbing the lead. Two days later the roles reversed, and the Gambits are back on top on game points, a reminder that in this six-round, mixed-team format, no lead survives long without a fight to defend it.

The day's headline match saw the Pipers take total control of their fixture against CheQ Mumba Masters with the white pieces. Magnus Carlsen beat Maxime Vachier-Lagrave, Anish Giri seized the initiative early against M Pranesh and never released it, and Volodar Murzin forced Bardiya Daneshvar to resign after 52 moves to seal an 11-6 win. Harika Dronavalli's victory over Divya Deshmukh was the only bright spot for the Mumba Masters.

Ganges Grandmasters piled more pain on the Continental Kings with a 15-3 rout, their second win over the same opponent. Polina Shuvalova, now with five wins from six games, beat Zhu Jiner to score four points on her board, Luke Leon Mendonca capitalized on an error from Pranav V on the prodigy board, and Stavroula Tsolakidou closed things out with a 63-move win over former Women's World Champion Alexandra Kosteniuk.

Mumba Masters had a chance to seize first place themselves against PBG Alaskan Knights and looked set to do it until the very end. Carissa Yip lost a winning position on time against Nino Batsiashvili, and Abhimanyu Mishra, still smarting from a heartbreaking loss the day before, beat Bardiya Daneshvar to hand his team the 11-6 win and pick up Player of the Match.

The decisive result of the night, though, came on the icon board in the Gambits-Kings match. Javokhir Sindarov, fresh off winning the 2026 Candidates, beat Alireza Firouzja convincingly while every other board finished drawn, turning what might have been a tight 6.5-6.5 split into an 8-5 win purely on the strength of one game.

It's a familiar pattern for Sindarov, who has already made headlines this GCL season for [his win over Anand](/articles/the-torch-passes-again-sindarov-s-win-over-anand-echoes-a-familiar-chess-story/) and for [belonging to Day One](/articles/carlsen-returns-but-gcl-day-one-belongs-to-sindarov/) despite Carlsen's return to the league.

Three teams now sit level on 12 match points, but game points separate them: the Gambits have 60 from seven matches, the Pipers 58 from six, and the Grandmasters 55 from six. That extra match played by the Gambits cuts both ways: it built their cushion, but leaves less room for error as the schedule tightens.

Thursday brings a heavyweight slate in Bengaluru, with the Pipers facing the Grandmasters at 3:30 PM, Mumba Masters against the Continental Kings at 5:00 PM, the Pipers again against PBG Alaskan Knights at 6:30 PM, and the Grandmasters closing the day against the league-leading Gambits at 8:00 PM. If Day 3 and Day 5 are any guide, whoever leads Thursday night shouldn't get too comfortable.

The 2026 Salesforce FIDE World Championship [begins today, Tuesday, November 24](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-fide-world-chess-championship-game-1), in Geneva. Defending champion Gukesh Dommaraju of India faces Uzbek challenger Javokhir Sindarov, with game one starting at 9 a.m. ET, 15:00 local time in Geneva and 7:30 p.m. IST.

Sindarov has White and will make the first move of the match. Judit Polgar and David Howell provide commentary on Chess.com/TV and on the Chess Twitch and YouTube channels.

**The money**

The prize fund is $2,500,000. Each game won is worth $200,000, and the money left over is split equally between the two players.

That structure rewards decisive games rather than the match result alone: every win adds $200,000 to a player's share, while draws leave the pot to be shared. For both players, the split guarantees a very large payday before a single result is counted.

**The format**

It is a 14-game match, and the first player to 7.5 points wins. The time control is two hours for 40 moves, then 30 minutes to the end of the game, with 30 seconds added per move from move 41 onward.

If the score reaches 7-7, a playoff follows. It starts with four games of 15+10 rapid chess. For readers new to the notation, our [time controls explainer](/articles/time-controls/) covers how increments like these work.

**The numbers going in**

Sindarov is rated 2782 and Gukesh 2710, a gap of 72 points in the challenger's favor. A gap like that is meaningful but far from decisive in a short classical match, where draws are common and a single game can swing things. Our [ratings explainer](/articles/chess-ratings/) covers what such a difference does and does not predict.

The pre-match mood has leaned toward the challenger. Carlsen called Sindarov a "significant favorite," and Nakamura and Carlsen both [picked Sindarov](/articles/kasparov-leans-gukesh-nakamura-and-carlsen-pick-sindarov/), while Kasparov leaned toward Gukesh. Gukesh, for his part, has vowed to win the title "all over again."

One reading is that White in game one gives Sindarov a small early initiative, though in a 14-game match no single colour assignment settles much. Our [pre-match look at the tone in Geneva](/articles/gukesh-and-sindarov-set-the-tone-before-game-1-in-geneva/) has more on how both players have framed the match.

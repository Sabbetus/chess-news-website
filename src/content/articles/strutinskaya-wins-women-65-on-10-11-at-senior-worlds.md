The 2026 FIDE World Senior Chess Championship [ended on November 14 in Vrnjačka Banja, Serbia](https://www.fide.com/world-senior-chess-champions-2026-crowned-in-vrnjacka-banja-serbia/), after 11 rounds. It drew 385 players from 65 countries across four categories: Open 50+, Open 65+, Women 50+ and Women 65+.

The closing ceremony opened with a minute of silence for Israeli International Master Leon Lederman. The organiser, the Chess Federation of Serbia, scaled back many of the planned ceremony activities as a result.

**The four podiums**

Galina Strutinskaya won the Women 65+ with 10/11, the biggest margin of any category. Irina Chelushkina of Serbia took silver on 7.5, and Elena Krasenkova of Poland took bronze, also on 7.5. Strutinskaya had been [perfect through the first five rounds](/articles/strutinskaya-is-5-5-at-senior-worlds-while-open-sections-bunch-up/), and she lost only a point and a half over the full distance.

Pia Cramling won the Women 50+ on 9/11. Monica Calzetta Ruiz of Spain was second on 8.5, and Maritza Arribas Robaina of Cuba third on 7.5.

The Open 50+ went to Murtas Kazhgaleyev of Kazakhstan on 8.5. Kiril Georgiev of Bulgaria and Kazakhstan's Petr Kostenko both finished on 8 for silver and bronze.

**A three-way tie in the Open 65+**

The Open 65+ was the tightest section. Zurab Sturua of Georgia, Keith Arkell of England and Alexander Mikhalevski of Israel all finished on 8.5/11, and the medals went in that order.

Three players level at the top of an 11-round Swiss is a good argument for the sort of [tiebreak rules](/articles/swiss-system-tiebreaks/) that decide such podiums. The published order shows Sturua first, Arkell second and Mikhalevski third.

**Honours for the 75+ group and five legends**

Special awards went to the 75+ group. Nona Gaprindashvili, Dušan Rajković, Hermaan Van Riemsdijk and Nathan Birnboim received medals from Bojana Bejatović, Secretary General of the Serbian federation.

Before Round 9, the Serbian federation also gave plaques to five figures. Gaprindashvili was recognised as a five-time World Champion who held the title from 1962 to 1978 and in 1978 became the first woman awarded the Grandmaster title.

Cramling, a two-time European women's champion who reached world number one among women in 1984, was honoured, and so was Chelushkina. She has won national women's titles in the Soviet Union, Ukraine, Yugoslavia, Serbia and Montenegro, and Serbia.

Ketevan Arakhamia-Grant, a former world junior girls' champion, and Branko Damljanović were the other two. Damljanović was part of the Yugoslav team that won silver at the 1989 World Team Championship and took individual bronze at the 1996 Olympiad. He has since served as captain and coach of Serbia's national team.

**Thanks from the organisers**

Tournament director Saša Jevtić thanked chief arbiter Ashot Vardapetyan and the organising team. FIDE representative Gunnar Bjornsson praised "fine fighting chess, sportsmanship, and the special spirit that only senior players bring to the board."

The event was played at Hotel Zepter. Trophies were handed out by representatives of the hotel, FIDE, the Vrnjačka Banja municipality and Serbian federation president Andrija Jorgić.

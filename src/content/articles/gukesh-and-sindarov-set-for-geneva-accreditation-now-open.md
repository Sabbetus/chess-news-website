FIDE has [opened media accreditation for the Salesforce FIDE World Championship](https://www.fide.com/media-accreditation-for-the-salesforce-fide-world-championship-is-now-open/), which starts on November 22, 2026, in Geneva, Switzerland. The match pits World Champion Gukesh D of India against Challenger Javokhir Sindarov of Uzbekistan, both 20 years old.

The first of 14 games is scheduled for November 24, so the opening day, November 22, comes two days before the first move. The notice does not say what happens on the days in between, but the gap suggests opening ceremonies and media events ahead of play.

**How to apply**

Journalists must complete the digital form on the match website, worldchampionship.fide.com/media. Applications are reviewed by FIDE's team, and approved applicants receive a separate confirmation email.

There will be no on-site registration desk at the venue. The online form is the only route to a pass.

Accreditation stays open throughout the championship. FIDE warns, however, that it cannot guarantee timely processing of last-minute applications, and urges media representatives to apply in advance.

**Who qualifies**

Press accreditation is reserved for professional journalists and representatives of bona fide media organisations. A limited number of passes may also go to independent writers, bloggers, YouTubers and freelance reporters.

That last category matters in chess, where a large share of the audience follows the game through streamers and independent commentators rather than traditional outlets. The word "limited" is FIDE's, and it implies that creators will be competing for a small allocation.

Returning press can expect the usual procedures and media policies. First-timers at a FIDE event are strongly advised to read the detailed media guidelines published by FIDE.

**The match itself**

Two players who are both 20 is a striking pairing for the sport's top title. For readers new to the format, our [World Chess Championship guide](/events/world-chess-championship/#how-it-works) covers how these matches work, and the [road to the title](/articles/world-championship-cycle/) explains how a challenger earns the right to play.

Whether the match between two same-age players produces a long, cautious classical grind or a sharper fight is the open question, and the 14-game length leaves room for either.

For anyone planning to cover it, the practical advice is simple: apply early, through the form, and do not count on sorting anything out at the door.

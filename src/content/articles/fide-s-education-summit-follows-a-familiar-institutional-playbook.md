FIDE is folding another standalone event into the calendar of the 46th Chess Olympiad. On 24 September 2026, the federation will hold the [FIDE Summit on Chess in Education 2026 at Silk Road by Minyoun in Samarkand](https://www.fide.com/every-lesson-counts-fide-summit-on-chess-in-education-comes-to-samarkand-this-september/), running alongside the Olympiad and its Congress and serving as the centerpiece of the 2026 Year of Chess in Education.

The theme is "Every Lesson Counts," and the guest list is deliberately broad. Organizers are inviting educators, policymakers, researchers and grassroots innovators to register, with the day hosted by videographer, actor and chess content creator Simon Bélanger, known online as @simonplayschess.

The schedule itself tells you how FIDE wants this to feel less like a ceremonial add-on and more like a working conference. After a lunch break, Part II runs from 14:00 to 15:30 as a set of shorter panel discussions covering established national programs, the struggles of emerging pilot initiatives, and new partnerships with universities and nonprofits.

Part III, from 16:00 to 18:00, is the meatiest stretch: six Education Innovation Lectures, each built around a single theme.

Topics include chess for refugee and conflict-affected communities, the history of FIDE's own education programming, how to make chess genuinely enjoyable for kids rather than a chore, chess as a tool for building community connections globally, results from a new Chess in Education Global Survey, and chess's impact outside the tournament hall entirely.

Logistics matter here too. Real-time voice translation will be offered in 64 languages, though attendees need to bring their own headphones, and registration is required for anyone attending in person. Those needing an official invitation letter for visa purposes are directed to Rita Atkins, Secretary of the FIDE Chess in Education Commission.

None of this is happening in a vacuum. FIDE has spent the past couple of years building out standing commissions and formal structures around constituencies it previously engaged more loosely, a pattern already visible in [the federation's new Athletes Commission, modeled loosely on the IOC's own body](/articles/fide-s-new-athletes-commission-echoes-the-ioc-s-playbook/).

Declaring a full "Year of Chess in Education" and staging a dedicated summit around it fits the same instinct: take something FIDE has long claimed as part of its mission and give it a calendar, a commission, a budget line and a stage.

The choice of venue reinforces that logic rather than complicating it. Piggybacking on the Olympiad guarantees a captive audience of federation officials, delegates and media already in Samarkand for the Congress, without the cost or risk of standing up a separate international gathering. It's the same reasoning that has made Olympiad weeks a magnet for side meetings on everything from anti-doping to ratings reform.

What's genuinely new is the scope of who's being asked to show up. This isn't a closed-door commission meeting; it's pitched at classroom teachers and school administrators as much as at federation bureaucrats, with six lecture slots dedicated entirely to practice rather than policy.

Whether that translates into anything durable, new funding streams, adopted curricula, or just a well-attended one-day event, is the open question hanging over 24 September. Registration is open now at edu-summit2026.fide.com.

Hikaru Nakamura [leads Clutch Chess: Champions Showdown 2026 by half a point](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-clutch-chess-champions-showdown-day-1) after day one at the Saint Louis Chess Club. He won two of his six games, lost none, and sits on 4/6.

GM Javokhir Sindarov is alone in second on 3.5 with one win and no losses. GM Fabiano Caruana has 2.5, and GM Praggnanandhaa Rameshbabu is last on 2.

**How the format works**

The event is three double round-robins, one per day, so everyone plays everyone with both colors each day. The 10+5 time control fits 12 games into a day.

Stakes climb daily. A win is worth $1,000 on day one, $2,000 and two points on day two, and $3,000 and three points on day three.

Drawn games feed a pool, the Champion's Jackpot, which is added to the winner's pot. Seven draws on day one put $7,000 into it.

For the full prize structure, see our earlier look at [how Clutch Chess pays $125,000 to win](/articles/clutch-chess-pays-125-000-to-win-with-points-rising-daily/).

**A lead that surprised its owner**

Nakamura said on the broadcast that the result ran against his expectations. He figured the others, who have been "super active," would "come out with guns blazing," while he hasn't played over the board "in I don't know how long."

Commentator GM Yasser Seirawan agreed. "I did not expect Hikaru, who skipped the Grand Chess Tour, to come out and look so good. But he's got to do it all again tomorrow."

The lead is thin. With wins worth two points on day two, half a point is easily erased. Even last-place Praggnanandhaa is only two points back, a single day-two win.

**The Caruana mini-match**

Nakamura's first win came against Caruana. Game one was drawn, though Nakamura was briefly winning a rook endgame and let it slip.

He made up for it in game two. The players followed the playoff game GM Wesley So won against Caruana at the U.S. Championship, the game that decided that match. This time Caruana chose the sounder 5...Qe7 rather than the dubious 5...0-0.

The queens came off and the position turned into a dry endgame. Nakamura outplayed Caruana with a bishop against a knight, rooks still on the board.

The critical moment came at move 44. 44...Nc4 was the only move, but Caruana played [44...Nf7](#game-embed), which left the knight on an undefended square. Tactically, that made all the difference.

Caruana was runner-up to Magnus Carlsen in the inaugural event last year. Carlsen and World Champion Gukesh Dommaraju are not in this edition. Praggnanandhaa and Sindarov replaced them.

Day two, covering rounds four to six, starts Wednesday, October 28, at 1 p.m. ET.

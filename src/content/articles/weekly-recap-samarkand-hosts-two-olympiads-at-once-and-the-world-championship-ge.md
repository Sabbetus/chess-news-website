Samarkand carried the week almost single-handedly, running the 3rd FIDE Chess Olympiad for People with Disabilities to its finish just as the 46th Chess Olympiad opened in the same city. Around that, FIDE nailed down the venue and a new sponsor for the 2026 World Championship match, and the usual glut of calendar data and online chess news kept things moving.

[The disability Olympiad opened with a record field](/articles/samarkand-s-disability-olympiad-hits-a-record-40-teams/), and Round 2 produced eight teams sitting on a perfect four match points, setting up direct collisions at the top of the table.

By Round 3, [Cuba, Israel and the Czech Republic had pulled clear on 6/6](/articles/samarkand-s-deaf-champion-coached-its-future-star/), with Round 4 pitting Cuba against Israel head to head and the Czechs facing Germany.

Cuba won that showdown. [Cuba beat Israel 2½–1½ to reach four wins from four matches](/articles/cuba-stays-perfect-as-the-disability-olympiad-nearly-doubles/), becoming the tournament's only unbeaten side.

That run didn't last. [Round 5 handed Cuba its first defeat of the event](/articles/cuba-s-unbeaten-run-ends-as-poland-and-czechia-share-the-lead/), lifting Poland and the Czech Republic into a share of the lead.

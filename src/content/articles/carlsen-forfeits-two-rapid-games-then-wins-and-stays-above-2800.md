At the Total Chess World Championship Tour Pilot in Budapest, Magnus Carlsen [forfeited both rapid games against Vladislav Artemiev](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-total-chess-world-championship-tour-pilot-day-9) on Thursday, then won the fast classic game on demand. The 11-9 set win kept his live rating at 2804 and extended a 17-year run above 2800.

Carlsen had won three of four blitz games, losing the other after blundering into mate in an endgame. At 3-1 up, he told Norway's TV 2 he would only come back for the fast classic: "If I have drawing odds or not, I could not care less."

**The arithmetic of a forfeit**

Each forfeited rapid game cost him four points, so Artemiev took both and Carlsen walked into the final game trailing by 6. A win was worth eight, which meant he had to win. A draw would have dropped him to 2799, and only if he lost while Caruana won could Caruana have taken world number one.

Carlsen said he wasn't confident, having not won a classical game all event. Artemiev reached move 21 with one minute against 26 for Carlsen. He sacrificed a pawn, defended well on the increment, and eventually collapsed.

Artemiev took it gracefully: "It's a logical result. Unfortunately, no chance today." It was Carlsen's first fast classic win of the tournament, after nine draws and a loss in the group stage and forfeits of both fast classic games against Bibisara Assaubayeva.

**The criticism**

Skipping games in decided matches had drawn some sympathy given the schedule. Doing it in an open set was harder to defend. Fabiano Caruana said: "I think you should just forfeit the match if you don't play."

Anish Giri, speaking before the latest forfeits, said: "I wouldn't dare to do it myself... I'm too old for these shenanigans." Hikaru Nakamura, after the Assaubayeva forfeits, argued that objections to regulations belong before an event starts.

As we noted when [Carlsen skipped a game with 2800 at stake](/articles/firouzja-and-giri-take-semifinal-leads-at-total-chess/), the format makes this a calculated gamble. It worked, and it may be that the result will only encourage more of it.

**Giri survives to lead the final**

In the final, Giri leads Alireza Firouzja 11-9 after the first set. Giri won two blitz games and drew two for a 3-1 lead. Firouzja won the first rapid game with White, and Giri answered by winning the second, using Firouzja's time trouble.

Chess.com noted Firouzja missed 38.g5!!, with the idea of 39.Be6! and a rook sacrifice for a mating attack.

The fast classic, worth eight points, was the [Game of the Day](#game-embed).

Firouzja, with White and needing a win, allowed doubled pawns to open the g-file.

Giri said: "I'm in trouble and then I think I was lost, but I was trying to put up a fight... Very lucky."

ChessBase says Firouzja's key mistake was offering a queen trade, letting Giri reach a knight and four pawns against a rook and a pawn ending. Giri held the draw.

**Elsewhere**

Caruana beat Maxime Vachier-Lagrave 11-9 in the third-place match, with two blitz wins and draws everywhere else. Giri and Caruana both had Black in the fast classic, so either would have White if needing a win Friday.

Hans Niemann beat Jan-Krzysztof Duda 13.5-6.5, unbeaten. Ian Nepomniachtchi led Le Quang Liem 14-6. Haik Martirosyan beat Arjun Erigaisi 14.5-5.5 after trailing 0.5-3.5 in blitz.

Richard Rapport led Jorden van Foreest 11-1 and forfeited the fast classic. Wesley So led Levon Aronian 12.5-7.5, and Praggnanandhaa Rameshbabu led Abhimanyu Mishra 12-8.

Firouzja must win Friday to force armageddon. Play starts at 9 a.m. ET / 15:00 CET.

Yu Yangyi won the Shenzhen Nanshan International Chess Masters 2026 by beating Ding Liren in an Armageddon game with the white pieces, after a final that [could not be separated until the second tiebreak stage](https://en.chessbase.com/post/shenzhen-nanshan-mastes-2026-report). Both finalists were seeded directly into the main draw, and the all-Chinese showdown went the distance.

**How the final unfolded**

The four rapid games (15 minutes plus 10 seconds per move) were all drawn. In the two-game blitz tiebreak (3 minutes plus 2 seconds), each player won one game, so the match stayed level.

That sent them to the decider: 5 minutes for White, 4 for Black, a 2-second increment only after move 60, and a draw counting as a win for Black. Yu had White, so he needed a victory, and [he got one](#game-embed). For readers new to the format, our explainer on [how time controls work](/articles/time-controls/) covers why an Armageddon game cannot end in a draw.

Wang Hao won the match for third place.

**The format**

Twelve players took part in a rapid knockout at the Qiuxian Pavilion in Shenzhen, starting 9 October. Four top grandmasters, Ding Liren, Yu Yangyi, Wang Hao and Xu Xiangyu, skipped the preliminary stage. Eight players born after 2000 played a preliminary event, and the top four advanced: Asian champion Kong Xiangrui, Xiao Tong, Huang Renjie and Meng Yihan.

Peng Xiongjian, Jiang Haochen and the two female players, Zhu Jiner and Yan Tianqi, went out in the preliminary stage, in some cases after closely contested matches.

**Quarterfinals and semifinals**

Each established grandmaster met one of the youngsters in the quarterfinals, and the veterans took three of four matches. Ding beat Meng Yihan 3-1. Yu beat Huang Renjie 2½-½, finishing after only three games.

Wang Hao had the scare. He drew the first game and lost the second against Xiao Tong, then won the third in a must-win spot to level the match, and took game four to advance.

Xu Xiangyu versus Kong Xiangrui was the only quarterfinal to need extra time. Each won one game and two were drawn. Xu lost the second blitz game and was out, leaving Kong as the only young player in the semifinals.

Kong's run ended there. Ding beat him 2½-½. Yu drew his first two games with Wang Hao, then won the last two rapid games to reach the final.

**What it suggests**

Yu's path was the cleaner of the two: he never needed a tiebreak until the final, and he beat Wang Hao in regulation after being held in the first two games. Ding, the former world champion, got through both his earlier matches in regulation as well, so the final was the first time either had to go to blitz.

Armageddon is a lottery with a thumb on the scale. White gets more time but must win, and Black can draw. Winning it with White, under that pressure, is a real mark in Yu's favour, though one decider says little about the wider gap between two players who split ten games' worth of play so evenly.

In the Round of 16 of the 2026 Speed Chess Championship, GM Alireza Firouzja [beat GM Vladislav Artemiev 11-7](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-speed-chess-championship-r16-firouzja-artemiev) and booked a Quarterfinal against the winner of Javokhir Sindarov vs. Fabiano Caruana. Firouzja essentially won the match in 75 minutes of 5+1, and what happened in the bullet section was stranger than the score.

**A 6-1 start in 5+1**

Firouzja won five of the seven 5+1 games. Artemiev lost the first on time after his position had already collapsed, and in the second he was down to five seconds against one minute 45 when he exchanged on d4 and was hit by [28...Ra2](#ply-56).

The third game, the Game of the Day, was closer. Artemiev gave up his queen for two rooks and had real winning chances, but Firouzja's trapped king marched up the board and he won.

Artemiev got a draw in the next game. In the one after, he missed a chance, ran low on time and lost to 37.Ng6, since taking the knight allows Re8+. Artemiev then got only a draw from a winning position before Firouzja ground out the fifth win.

Firouzja said he stayed on the board rather than the clock: "I know his style from many years—he's always in time trouble; it's kind of a Grischuk style." He added that he had expected 5+1 to be closer than it was.

**3+1: a lead protected**

Artemiev did win a game in 3+1 when Firouzja blundered in a complex position. Firouzja drew the next and restored a five-point lead.

The pivotal game came after 21...e3?, when Artemiev had one move to grab a win almost on the spot. Instead both sides blundered, he went on to lose, and the lead became six points. Two draws left it there, and Firouzja won the section 4-3.

**Bullet as a clock exercise**

Firouzja was the Bullet Chess Champion in 2024 and 2025 and the 2026 runner-up, yet he chose not to race. "I was six points up going to bullet, and in 25 minutes he has mathematical chances, of course—so I have to do the professional thing!"

That meant quiet endgames, played out as long as possible. The first 1+1 game reached move 194 with a rook and three pawns each, and ended in a draw.

Artemiev contributed by pushing a pawn, which reset the count toward a claimable draw. That one game took 10 of the 25 minutes.

The second game lasted 101 moves. In the third, Firouzja blundered a piece on move 17 but still dragged it to move 75, waiting over 20 seconds in the final position to lose on time rather than allow mate in one.

Artemiev won the fourth, but the match clock ran out after only four bullet games, a record low for 1+1. He took the section 3-1, which mattered only for prize money. Firouzja earned $6,545.14 and Artemiev $1,579.86.

**Context and what comes next**

The pre-match prediction made Firouzja a 93 percent favorite, which GMs Aman Hambleton and Eric Hansen thought far too high. Firouzja himself called the pairing "very bad" for a first round. Artemiev had not played the Speed Chess Championship since 2020, while Firouzja is bidding for a third straight final.

Before the match, Firouzja was third and Artemiev ninth on the Champions Chess Tour leaderboard, which decides 12 qualifiers for the 2027 Esports World Cup. The Speed Chess Championship is one of three events counting toward it, alongside the Titled Tuesday Grand Prix and next year's Chess.com Open.

The marathon games raised the question of whether the one-second increment should go. Firouzja, who recently won the [Total Chess Pilot](/articles/firouzja-wins-total-chess-pilot-after-drawing-armageddon-with-black/), has no objection: "I think it makes sense to remove the increment because we play online anyway."

He joins GMs Denis Lazavik and Hikaru Nakamura in the Quarterfinals. All three reached the live Finals of the last edition, and the winners of this round play the Semifinals in London next February.

The next Round of 16 match, GMs Wesley So vs. Praggnanandhaa Rameshbabu, is on Wednesday, December 9, at 10 a.m. ET.

Round three of the 46th Chess Olympiad in Samarkand mostly went to script, but the exceptions were sharp enough to reshape a few storylines heading into the middle rounds. Most seed favorites [held serve on the top boards](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-samarkand-chess-olympiad-round-3), yet two results in particular stood out among the top 50 pairings.

The biggest was Kyrgyzstan's 2.5-1.5 win over Brazil. Kyrgyzstan came into the round seeded 79th, Brazil 31st, a 48-place gap that makes this one of the more eye-catching scalps of the event so far. A seed gap that wide usually means a routine match for the higher-ranked side, which is exactly why this result is worth flagging rather than filing away as a routine 2.5-1.5.

The other notable upset came from Singapore, seeded 50th, which beat 21st-seeded Norway. That's a smaller gap than the Kyrgyzstan result but still a real shock on paper, especially given the depth Norway typically fields.

Both results are reminders that Olympiad pairings compress quickly once you get outside the top ten or twenty seeds, and a single off day from a favorite's board one or two can flip a match entirely.

The Netherlands avoided becoming a third name on that list thanks to Anish Giri.

Giri's result on his board proved decisive in salvaging the match for the Dutch team, a reminder of why a strong top board still matters even in an event increasingly defined by depth and bench strength, a theme that has run through this Olympiad since [Erigaisi's loss didn't stop India's bench from holding firm](/articles/erigaisi-falls-but-india-s-bench-holds-at-samarkand/) earlier in the tournament.

The women's section produced its own upsets. Argentina beat Ukraine, and Iran beat France, both results that will register as genuine surprises given the pedigree of the two European sides on the losing end. Coming in the same round as the open section's shocks, it adds to a picture of an Olympiad where seeding has been a looser guide than usual through the early rounds.

That pattern has been building since the first days in Samarkand, when [El Salvador's 94th seed stunned Cuba](/articles/el-salvador-s-94th-seed-stuns-cuba-at-samarkand/) and later when [Liang's shock loss rattled the field](/articles/liang-s-shock-loss-and-montenegro-s-draw-rattle-samarkand/) in another round. Round three fits that same mold rather than breaking it.

For Kyrgyzstan and Singapore, the immediate reward is momentum and a seeding bump into the next pairing cycle, where they'll likely face stronger opposition than the underdog slot they occupied here.

For Brazil and Norway, the cost is mostly reputational at this stage of a long Swiss event, a bad round that's recoverable if the bench responds, but a genuine dent in a tournament being played in front of a record field.

With over 400 teams entered at Samarkand, there's little margin for a team to treat any round as a formality, no matter how lopsided the seeding looks on paper.

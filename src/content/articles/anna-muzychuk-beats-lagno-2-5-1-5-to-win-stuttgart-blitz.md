GM Anna Muzychuk [won the blitz portion of the WR Women's Chess Tour Grand Final 2026](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-wr-womens-chess-tour-grand-final-day-1) in Stuttgart, beating GM Kateryna Lagno 2.5-1.5 in the final. That earns her 10 points and a two-point lead over Lagno. The rapid knockout, played today, Monday 23 November, still decides the overall winner.

**How the event works**

The Grand Final closes a five-month tour that began in Japan in June, then went to the Dominican Republic, Mozambique and France. Two players from each stop qualified, making an eight-player field. The games are at Rosenstein Palace, a former summer palace of King Wilhelm I of Wurttemberg that now houses Stuttgart's natural history museum.

Day one was a single-elimination blitz at 3+2, with each match best of four games. Day two repeats the knockout in rapid at 15+10, best of two games per match.

Points go 10 for first, 8 for second, 5 each for third and fourth, and 2 each for fifth through eighth, and the combined total picks the champion. The prize fund is $40,000, with $7,000 to the winner.

One seat changed hands: GM Bibisara Assaubayeva, who qualified by winning the rapid section in France, declined her invitation to join GM Javokhir Sindarov for the 2026 World Championship. GM Mariya Muzychuk replaced her, so both Muzychuk sisters play.

**Sisters first, then the top seed**

The draw put the sisters together in the quarterfinals. Anna won 2.5-1.5, taking games two and four with White after an opening draw. In game four Mariya blundered the f-pawn, and the knight that took it later trapped a rook on d3.

Mariya joked that they had flown to Germany just to play each other. Anna called that first round especially "unpleasant." They rarely play decisive games, but the knockout left no room for draws.

Elsewhere in the round, top seed GM Zhu Jiner beat IM Carissa Yip 2.5-1.5, after Yip, a piece up and winning in game one, traded rooks and lost. IM Leya Garifullina beat IM Mai Narva 2.5-0.5, and Lagno beat GM Vaishali Rameshbabu 2.5-0.5.

Lagno's opener came with four seconds against 29 on the clock, when Vaishali played 24.Kh2?? and walked into a mating attack. The only move was 24.Ra4.

**Semifinals**

Muzychuk beat Zhu in four games: a win, a loss, a draw, then a win. The last looked drawn, a knight endgame with four pawns each, until Zhu blundered a piece.

Lagno lost game one to Garifullina, then won games two and three and drew the fourth. In game two Garifullina was a pawn up and winning at one point, then lost on time in an equal rook endgame. In game three she resigned one move before checkmate.

**The final**

Muzychuk, from Ukraine, won the first two games against Lagno, who is Russian. The players did not shake hands.

In game one Lagno played an unsound pawn sacrifice. In game two's time scramble she [walked into a permanent pin and lost the exchange](#game-embed).

Lagno pulled one back in game three. Muzychuk said she only thought about her own plan and didn't consider her opponent's, which happens in blitz. Her 16.Qd2 telegraphed the deadly bishop sacrifice.

In game four, Muzychuk only needed a draw and had White. They played the Four Knights, the queens came off by move 11, and she drew comfortably.

**What's next**

Lagno, a three-time Women's World Blitz Champion, is the obvious threat in the rapid section, as she sits two points back. A two-point gap is small: the rapid scale pays 10 for the title and 8 for second, so a rapid win for Lagno with Muzychuk finishing second would put them level.

The Muzychuk sisters can only meet again in the rapid final. Play starts at 5:00 a.m. ET, 11:00 CET.

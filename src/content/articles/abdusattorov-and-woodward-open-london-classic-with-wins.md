Two wins and three draws opened the 2026 London Chess Classic Masters. According to [Chess.com's round-one report](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-london-chess-classic-round-1), defending champion Nodirbek Abdusattorov beat 17-year-old Shreyas Royal with White, while 16-year-old Andy Woodward took down Ihor Samunenkov with Black.

Both winners sit on 1/1 with eight rounds still to play, so the lead means little yet. Still, the tournament is a single round-robin of 10 players, and a point on day one is a point nobody else can easily take back.

**Abdusattorov bulldozes Royal**

The commentators named Abdusattorov's win the game of the day. It began as an English Opening but by move 15 had the shape of a Benoni Defense with reversed colors.

His energetic [21.c5](#game-embed) looked great over the board and worked, even though the computer finds some remarkable resources for Black. In practice the initiative swept Royal off his feet.

Abdusattorov won this event last year by two full points over Alireza Firouzja, a margin that is rare in a field this strong. Opening with a clean win against the youngest Englishman in the field suggests he is picking up where he left off.

**Woodward finds the c-file**

Woodward's game was a Moscow Variation of the Sicilian Defense. He chose the combative 3...Nd7, and by move 12 the position looked like a Najdorf.

As often happens when Black does well in the Sicilian, he built heavy pressure down the half-open c-file. The win came before Abdusattorov's finished.

**Three draws, with chances**

Vasyl Ivanchuk drew with Nihal Sarin, and the Ukrainian had an extra pawn at one point. He said he was "pretty happy" with his preparation, and the players followed a 2006 Magnus Carlsen game for the first 19 moves. Nihal's defensive pawn sacrifice 21...d5?! held up, though Ivanchuk had chances.

"Okay, hard to be happy because I was clearly better out of the opening, but okay, my opponent defended well," Ivanchuk said.

Levon Aronian had a better pawn structure with White against Luke McShane in a Four Knights Spanish, but found no concrete way to use it. Gawain Jones against Michael Adams was the more dramatic of the two draws.

Adams had a serious chance to win with Black and ended up with a pawn for the exchange. The last error came on move 40, when Adams had about three minutes left before the extra half hour arrived.

**The event**

This is the fourth edition since 2020 of the London Chess Classic, first held in 2009. It has been annual since 2023 after a three-year gap caused by COVID-19. The time control is 90 minutes for 40 moves, then 30 minutes, with a 30-second increment from move one.

The field mixes generations: Adams is 55 and Ivanchuk 57, against Woodward at 16. The tournament runs November 25 to December 4 at the Novotel West in Hammersmith, and the opening ceremony was held at the Tower of London. Round two starts today, Thursday, at 11 a.m. ET.

In the Challengers group, Mateusz Bartel, Rasmus Svane and Carissa Yip lead after round one.

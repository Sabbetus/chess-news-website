The Salesforce FIDE World Championship Match runs in Geneva from 22 November to 12 December, with reigning champion Gukesh Dommaraju defending against challenger Javokhir Sindarov. Alongside the games, FIDE has [announced a programme of side activities](https://www.fide.com/where-chess-meets-its-fans-join-side-activities-of-the-world-championship-match/) aimed at spectators who want more than a seat in the hall.

The idea is simple enough. A three-week match leaves long stretches where the board action is slow and the audience is waiting, and a roster of elite players in town can fill that time.

**The programme**

It opens on 24 November, when Viswanathan Anand shares memories and insights from his own World Championship journeys. Some of the stories will be familiar from his memoir Master Mind, but FIDE pitches the session as a chance to hear them first-hand.

Anand also appears in a more hands-on role: FIDE notes that spectators can play against him. The announcement does not give the format or date of that activity.

Swiss players carry much of the load. Alexandra Kosteniuk, the 12th Women's World Champion, and Grandmaster Yannick Pelletier will lead a range of activities through the event.

Two are named. Kosteniuk's audience challenge takes place during Game 1, and Pelletier's fan blitz session runs during Game 3.

**Tandem simul in Game 4**

The most distinctive item is the tandem simultaneous exhibition during Game 4. Grandmaster Anna Muzychuk, one of the few women ever to surpass the 2600 rating mark, will team up with Anish Giri for it.

Giri is known for his sharp, often self-deprecating public presence, which should suit a format built for the crowd. Muzychuk and Giri will also take part in other events on other days.

**Why scheduling around games makes sense**

Putting the activities alongside specific games is a practical choice. Fans already in the venue or the city can fill the hours of a round with a live exhibition or blitz session instead of only watching a screen.

It also fits how recent World Championships have worked. FIDE points out that every recent edition has had a side programme, and Geneva follows that pattern. For an overview of how the match itself fits into the title cycle, see our [World Chess Championship guide](/events/world-chess-championship/#how-it-works).

**Registering**

Many activities require advance registration, and FIDE warns that places can run out. The side events calendar is expected to gain new additions, so FIDE suggests bookmarking it. Details and sign-ups are at tickets.fide.com/en/events.

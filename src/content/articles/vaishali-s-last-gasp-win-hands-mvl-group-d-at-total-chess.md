The group stage of the Total Chess World Championship Tour Pilot in Budapest [ended with Firouzja, Caruana, Giri and Vachier-Lagrave winning their groups](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-total-chess-world-championship-tour-pilot-day-6).

The Semifinals are Vachier-Lagrave vs. Giri and Caruana vs. Firouzja, starting Tuesday, November 17, after a rest day. Scoring in the fast classical games was three points for a win and one for a draw, with each pair playing twice with colors reversed.

**Group D: Vaishali decides it**

Three players were tied for first going into the final day, but only Vachier-Lagrave controlled his own fate, thanks to the best blitz tiebreak. He drew the first game with Bibisara Assaubayeva, then won the second with White in an endgame.

That left the result to Vaishali Rameshbabu and Arjun Erigaisi. Arjun won the first game, which put him in sole lead. [Vaishali won the return game](#game-embed) after it swung both ways, and Arjun slipped to third on 16 points.

Vachier-Lagrave finished on 17. Hans Niemann took second on 16, ahead of Arjun on the blitz tiebreak. He had lost the first game to Wesley So after blundering with 59...f4?, then beat So with White in the return.

So, now fourth on 13, offered an apology for calling Niemann "the most disrespectful teenager in chess" years ago at the U.S. Championship, adding "He's a good kid!" He then criticized the knockout format, tweeting that eight games a day at different time controls over four days is "maximum absurdity."

**Group B: Caruana wins, then loses**

Caruana had the group sewn up once Le Quang Liem drew with Praggnanandhaa in the first game of the day. He then said he was "messing around a bit" against Artemiev in the second game to keep winning chances. Artemiev, winless until then, won it.

The loss cost Caruana 6.8 rating points and dropped him below Hikaru Nakamura on the live list. Nakamura is again world number two, 9.2 points behind Carlsen. Caruana finished on 17 points, three clear of Le, who stayed unbeaten with two wins and eight draws.

**Group C: Carlsen winless**

Carlsen finished fifth on nine points, nine draws and one defeat, and lost 21.8 rating points.

In the first game against Nepomniachtchi he took big risks in the opening, built his biggest advantage of the event and then, as so often, ran short of time. He missed a chance to grab the pawn on b2 and after 26...Bd8?! 27.Rh2 the game was balanced again.

Had he lost it, his 15-year run as world number one could have ended. Nepomniachtchi, who had a clock edge, forced a draw. The second game was also drawn, and Nepomniachtchi took second on the blitz tiebreak at 13 points.

Giri won the group on 15 with two draws against Mamedyarov. He called the format "a fun time control" but questioned rating it as classical. He also mocked the sudden defenders of classical chess: "Where were they when we needed them so much?"

**Group A: Firouzja holds on**

Firouzja drew twice with Levon Aronian to finish on 15 points. Duda had won the first game against Jorden van Foreest, but Van Foreest, content with a draw, won the return with White. Both ended on 14, with Duda second on the blitz tiebreak.

Abdusattorov, the world number five, finished last on nine. Praggnanandhaa, world number seven, was last in Group B on seven, and the two now meet in the Semifinals of the knockout for 21st place.

Carlsen, meanwhile, plays Assaubayeva in the knockout for 17th place. If he does not win those games, Nakamura's gap could shrink quickly.

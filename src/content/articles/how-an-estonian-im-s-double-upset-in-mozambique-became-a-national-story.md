Mai Narva doesn't often make international news outside chess circles, but this week she made front pages back home. Estonia's national broadcaster ERR ran a story on the 40th-ranked player in the world stunning the world number four, and the framing tells you something about how rare this kind of result is.

The four-time Estonian Women's Champion [beat GM Zhu Jiner in both the rapid and blitz finals](https://www.chess.com/news/view/narva-zhu-2026-wr-womens-chess-tour-africa) of the WR Women's Chess Tour - Africa 2026 in Maputo, Mozambique, walking away with $14,000 and a guaranteed seat at the Grand Final in Stuttgart, Germany later this year. Zhu still qualifies too, but had to settle for $4,000 after finishing runner-up in the rapid.

**The numbers behind the upset**

The gap here wasn't small. Narva sits 40th in the world in women's classical chess (2405) and 40th in rapid (2352), while Zhu is ranked fourth in classical (2550) and sixth in rapid (2470), a roughly 150-point classical edge that made her the heavy favorite going in. To even reach the final, Narva first had to beat three-time African junior champion Lina Nassr of Algeria 2-0 in the quarterfinal, then five-time African women's champion Shrook Wafa of Egypt 1.5-0.5 in the semifinal.

Rating suggested that Zhu Jiner would dominate, but Mai Narva had something to say about that! Narva took $7,000 and already booked her spot in the Finals, while Zhu had to settle for $4,000. The rapid final came down to nerve as much as material: Narva drew the first game with white, then won the second with black to seal the match.

The blitz final the next day, back at Maputo's Radisson Blu Hotel, was even tighter and just as emphatic. Narva completed the sweep by beating Zhu 3-2 in an armageddon decider, according to the tour's own social media recap of the event. Across the whole tournament, the remaining six players in the field managed to score just three draws against them, a reminder of just how far ahead of the rest of the Maputo field both finalists were, even though the final result flipped the expected order between the two of them.

**How the tour actually runs**

The WR Women's Chess Tour is built as a tournament series that spans four continents, a season-long qualifying path into a Grand Final. Other coverage of the tour describes each two-day leg as an eight-player knockout, rapid on day one at a 15+10 time control with best-of-two matches, blitz on day two at 3+2 with best-of-four matches, and armageddon breaking any tie. The winner of each discipline earns $7,000 and a Grand Final berth.

Maputo was the third of four legs, following Tokyo in June and Punta Cana in July, with [Saint-Tropez, France](/articles/how-anna-muzychuk-went-from-boycotting-saudi-arabia-to-booking-a-spot-in-saint-t/) starting August 25 as the fourth. GMs Vaishali Rameshbabu and Kateryna Lagno were the winners of the event in Tokyo, IMs Carissa Yip and Leya Garifullina triumphed in Punta Cana, so Narva and Zhu's Maputo sweep now fills six of the eight Grand Final slots ahead of the trip to France.

**Why this one traveled further than most tour stops**

Tour legs happen roughly every month and don't always break out of chess-specific media. This one did, partly because Estonia rarely produces headline results against the world's top four, and partly because the story had a built-in twist: the heavy favorite lost twice, in the same city, in the same week, in two different formats, to a player rated over a hundred points below her.

For Estonian fans, a national outlet covering an IM beating a GM ranked fourth in the world is a rare kind of validation, the sort of moment that gets shared well past ordinary chess forums. For the wider chess audience, it's a reminder that rating gaps this size are no guarantee once the clocks get fast, and a knockout format with armageddon tiebreaks seems almost designed to produce exactly this kind of upset.

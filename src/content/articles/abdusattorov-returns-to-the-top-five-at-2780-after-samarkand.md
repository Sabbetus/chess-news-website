The 46th Chess Olympiad in Samarkand left its mark on the rating tables. FIDE's [October 2026 list](https://www.fide.com/fide-october-2026-rating-list-published/) was shaped primarily by that event, with shifts in both the Open and Women's top 10.

The headline mover is Nodirbek Abdusattorov. He is back in the top five after a three-month absence, and his Board 1 individual gold in Samarkand earned him 18 points. That lifts him to 2780, which equals his career-high rating.

For a player of his style, quick and fearless in rapid and blitz, a classical rating that matches his peak is the more telling number. Gains like 18 points at that level usually need a long unbeaten run against strong opposition, and an Olympiad on home soil supplied one. His team's path to gold is told in our look inside [Uzbekistan's gold-medal team](/articles/the-anti-motivator-and-the-silent-killer-inside-uzbekistan-s-gold-medal-team/).

## Yakubboev crosses 2700

Abdusattorov was not the only Uzbek to gain. Nodirbek Yakubboev added 27 points at home and crossed 2700 for the first time in his career, finishing at 2712. It was one of the ten largest gains among the top 100 players, Open and Women combined.

His Olympiad is remembered for [a knight fork that sent Uzbekistan into sole lead](/articles/yakubboev-s-knight-fork-sends-uzbekistan-into-sole-lead/). The new rating is the lasting record of that form.

## The youngsters keep climbing

Just below the Open top 10, Yağız Kaan Erdoğmuş gained 15 points at the Olympiad and made his debut in the world's top 20. The Turkish youngster has been rising rapidly, and this is the first time he has appeared in that group.

On the Women's side, Alice Lee earned 14 points and entered the Women's top 20 for the first time. Zhu Jiner collected 9 points in Samarkand and reclaimed second place on the Women's list.

## The biggest gains

The top-100 gain table is dominated by women. Armenia's Mariam Mkrtchyan (WGM, 2421) was the largest gainer of the period at +47, and her Samarkand result carried her past 2400. Mobina Alinasab (WGM, Iran, 2364) also gained 47, but hers came from the Festival International de l'Apogée in Nîmes-Caissargues-Pont du Gard rather than the Olympiad.

The rest of the list:

- Elnaz Kaliakhmet (WIM, Kazakhstan, 2383): +36
- Melika Mohammadi (WIM, Iran, 2378): +36
- Deysi Cori T. (IM, Peru, 2346): +30
- Lu Miaoyi (IM, China, 2400): +29
- Bodhana Sivanandan (WGM, England, 2382): +28
- Nodirbek Yakubboev (GM, Uzbekistan, 2712): +27
- Savitha Shri B (IM, India, 2405): +23
- Oliwia Kiolbasa (IM, Poland, 2405): +19

## A new 2400 class

The 2400 club added two more newcomers in September. Lu Miaoyi and Savitha Shri B both cleared the barrier thanks to Samarkand, with gains of 29 and 23 points. Mkrtchyan also crossed 2400 there.

Then there is Bodhana Sivanandan. At 11, she became the youngest Woman Grandmaster in history, and her 28-point gain in Samarkand helped get her there. We covered the milestone when it happened: [Bodhana Sivanandan, 11, Becomes Youngest WGM in History](/articles/bodhana-sivanandan-11-becomes-youngest-wgm-in-history/).

Olympiad rating gains tend to favor players on the way up, since a lower-rated player who holds her own against stronger opposition collects points quickly. Many of the biggest names on this list fit that description. Whether they can hold those numbers is the question for the next few months of play.

The full list is available on [FIDE's ratings site](https://ratings.fide.com/).

Wesley So spent his 33rd birthday [beating Sam Shankland in the first round](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-us-chess-championship-round-1) of the 2026 U.S. Chess Championship in St. Louis. So is a three-time national champion, and a win on opening day is a useful start in a short round-robin.

**Other Open winners**

Jeffery Xiong beat Sam Sevian, and Awonder Liang beat Hans Niemann. Xiong is the only new face in the field, replacing Dariusz Swiercz from last year's lineup. A first-round win for the newcomer is a good way to settle in.

Liang was not impressed with his own performance. "It was a pretty bad game overall, the worst game of both sections... We both were missing everything," he said afterwards.

The game did contain a number of small mistakes by both sides. The biggest was Liang's missed chance to win Niemann's queen, so his candour looks earned. A win is a win, and the scoretable doesn't record the quality of the play.

**Draws at the top**

Levon Aronian against Fabiano Caruana and Abhimanyu Mishra against Ray Robson both ended in draws without incident. Caruana, the favourite, is [chasing a fifth straight title](/articles/caruana-chases-a-fifth-straight-us-title-and-reshevsky-s-record/), and a draw against Aronian costs him little. He starts a half point behind the three winners, which is a gap he can close quickly.

**Women's section**

The Women's Championship produced four decisive games. The most striking was Irina Krush dropping her queen in a clearly better position, which let WIM Rachael Li win. It is the kind of blunder that stays with a player, and Krush will have to recover quickly with the second round only a day away.

IM Anna Zatonskih and FMs Thalia Cervantes and Megan Paragua also began with wins.

**Stakes and format**

The base prize fund is $250,000, identical to last year. Bonus prizes are on offer for finishing first outright, for a number of wins in a row, and for a strong finish. Those bonuses favour players who win games, which may encourage fighting chess over safe draws.

The event is also the U.S. qualifier for the next World Cup, as the official tournament for the U.S. FIDE zone. That gives the lower half of the table something to play for beyond the title.

Dr. Jeanne Cairns Sinquefield rang the bell at the Saint Louis Chess Club to start play, and two weeks of chess lie ahead. Round 2 for both sections is on Saturday, October 10, at 1 p.m. ET.

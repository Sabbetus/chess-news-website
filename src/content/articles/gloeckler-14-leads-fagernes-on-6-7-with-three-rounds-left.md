Fourteen-year-old German IM Christian Gloeckler [beat Austrian IM Lukas Dotzer in round seven](https://en.chessbase.com/post/fagernes-autumn-2026-r7) of the Fagernes Autumn Tournament and moved to 6/7, keeping sole first place in the GM Swiss. Kazakh GM Rinat Jumabayev is half a point behind on 5½ after beating top seed Elham Amar.

**How the win came**

Dotzer played the Sicilian, and the players castled on opposite sides. Gloeckler went for a familiar attacking plan, and [his kingside offensive eventually decided the game](#game-embed).

Dotzer had been among the players trailing the leader, so the direct meeting mattered twice over: Gloeckler gained a point on a rival and widened the gap behind him. Jumabayev was the only member of the chasing group to win in the round.

**The road to 6/7**

Gloeckler was the only player to start 3/3, after the six-way tie that followed round two [was broken in round three](/articles/six-players-share-the-lead-after-two-rounds-in-fagernes/). Tuesday's double round, the event's only one, brought his first grandmasters.

He drew Jumabayev in 84 moves, agreeing a draw in a rook endgame that was objectively drawn even though Gloeckler had an extra pawn. Later that day he beat third seed Frode Olav Olsen Urkedal, which left him on 4½/5.

Round six was another stern test, with Black against Amar, the highest-rated player in the field. The Norwegian got the more promising endgame, but the advantage faded and the game was drawn.

Those two draws against the strongest names in the field, one in a long grind and one from the black side, are probably as useful to his norm hopes as the wins.

**What is at stake**

Gloeckler is chasing another grandmaster norm. His first came earlier this year at the Biel Chess Festival, where he scored 8/9 in the Master Open with a performance rating of 2688. For background on how norms lead to the title, see our explainer on [how a player becomes a grandmaster](/articles/chess-titles/).

Jumabayev and Gloeckler have already met, so they cannot face each other in the last three rounds. That removes the one game that could have settled the lead head to head. It leaves the half-point margin to be defended or erased against other opposition, and a single slip by either leader would reshuffle the top.

**Format and schedule**

The GM group runs ten rounds over nine days at the Scandic Valdres hotel in Fagernes, about 187 kilometres north of Oslo. The tournament is played from 3 to 11 October. The Tuesday double round is part of a schedule meant partly to give players more flexibility when chasing title norms.

The final round is on Sunday 11 October.

Among the others, 10-year-old Indian talent Sarbartho Mani has 4/7. He arrives from a 4/9 score in Group B of the Legends & Prodigies Tournament in Madrid.

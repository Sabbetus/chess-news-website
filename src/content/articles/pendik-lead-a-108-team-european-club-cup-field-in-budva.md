The European Club Cup runs from 17 to 23 October, and [108 teams are entered in the open section](https://en.chessbase.com/post/european-club-cup-2026-preview), a few more than last year, with 22 more in the women's event. The venue changed at short notice. Hotel Medun in Herceg Novi needed sudden work, so the organisers moved everything to a holiday resort on the Grdvica River in Budva, Montenegro.

**The open favourites**

Bayegan Pendik Chess Sports Club top the starting list at an average of 2715. Anish Giri plays board one, and the roster includes Amin Tabatabaei, Shakhriyar Mamedyarov and Richard Rapport. The Turkish talents Yagiz Kaan Erdogmus and Ediz Gürel are also on the team, as is Volodar Murzin, who plays under the FIDE flag. Pendik finished third last year.

Alkaloid of North Macedonia are second at 2686. They lean on players from India and China, with Arjun Erigaisi and Wei Yi on the top boards. They were runners-up last year. Both clubs carry sponsor names: Alkaloid is a pharmaceutical company, Bayegan works in petrochemicals.

Defending champions SuperChess Romania are third at 2682 and lose Gukesh Dommaraju, who has what ChessBase calls an important appointment coming up. Nodirbek Abdusattorov steps in as team leader on board one.

Novy Bor of the Czech Republic, three-time winners and last year's bronze medallists, are fourth at 2659. Vincent Keymer scored 5/6 for them last year but is absent. Nihal Sarin and Pentala Harikrishna lead the team instead. Tuxera Aquaprofit Nagykanizsai of Hungary are fifth at 2633, with Matthias Bluebaum on board two as the highest-rated German in the field.

**A big gap behind the top five**

The seeding drops sharply after fifth place. Vugar Gashimov and Elektroprivreda are both at 2573, 60 points below Nagykanizsai. That suggests a five-team contest for the medals on paper, though a seven-round event with this many teams leaves room for upsets.

Seven rounds is short for a field this size. A big [Swiss](/articles/swiss-system-tiebreaks/) can only sort so much, so the top seeds may not all meet each other, and a single bad match could decide the medals.

That is also the appeal for amateurs. The event has the feel of an Olympiad, but it is played over six boards and open to clubs rather than national teams. Each federation may enter five teams, or four if it holds no national championship. Amateurs can face professionals or sit near them.

Germany has six clubs, only two from the Bundesliga: Schachfreunde Berlin and St. Pauli, both without professionals. Schachfreunde Berlin are seeded 15th at 2421.

Players may be registered in several leagues at once, so clubs bid for them. At the Club Cup a player can represent only one team, and ChessBase notes the choice usually follows the best financial offer. That helps explain why Giri, Wei Yi and Abdusattorov are the three highest-rated names in the field, with the money presumably following them.

**Women's section**

Cercle d'échecs de Monte-Carlo are the clear favourites at 2477, with a record nine Cup wins, including last year. Elisabeth Paehtz, a long-time member, plays again. Sirmium Sremska Mitrovica (2440) and SuperChess Romania (2400) are the closest challengers.

Matches in the women's event are played over four boards. Two German clubs are in the field. SG Löberitz include Dana Reizniece-Ozola, the former Latvian minister who now works for FIDE. Klub Kölner Schachfreunde field streamers Katharina Reinicke and Hannah Sayce, plus Anna Maia Kazarian and Anna Kantane.

Pairings for round one will set the tone in Budva, where the long field of club sides meets the professionals.

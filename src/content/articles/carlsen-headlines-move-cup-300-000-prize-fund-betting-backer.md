The inaugural MOVE Cup, an eight-player rapid event in Barcelona on January 19-20, 2027, [will be headlined by Magnus Carlsen](https://www.chess.com/news/view/carlsen-move-cup-barcelona-2027). The prize fund is €300,000, and games are played at 10 minutes plus a one-second increment.

Carlsen is the only confirmed player. The other seven grandmasters, expected to be among the top names in the world, will be announced in the coming weeks.

**Who is paying**

The event is backed by BETBY, a sportsbook technology supplier that has sponsored Carlsen since 2024. It runs alongside ICE Barcelona, a trade show for the gaming industry, and a charity hand-and-brain event on January 18 comes first.

That setting tells you where the money is coming from. A betting-technology firm is putting its sponsored star in front of an industry audience in a city that organizers say is guaranteed to host the tournament for the next three years.

Organizers are also pairing the prize fund with the new MOVE Foundation, which receives a €300,000 contribution matching the players' prize money. Any extra money raised during the event is to be split equally between the prize fund and the foundation.

Its stated priorities are chess development, support for young talent, and research into chess and mental health. Board member Inese Dabola said it would work with existing projects as well as develop its own, with information about donations and activities published online.

**The format**

Day one is a round-robin among the eight players, and the top four reach the semifinals. Semifinals are best of three games, and the final is best of five.

Carlsen helped design the format, and the increment was his call. "One second is just about enough to physically make the moves," he said. Two seconds, he argued, lets players accumulate time, and he suggested 1.5 seconds would be ideal.

For spectators on site, there will be live commentary through headphones and scores on large screens, similar to Chess.com's Speed Chess Championship Finals and the Esports World Cup Final. Carlsen said the point is to fix what he sees as a gap: "we kind of lack events that bring chess to an audience that can have a better spectator experience."

**The Tata Steel clash**

The dates overlap with Tata Steel Chess, which runs January 15-31, 2027 in Wijk aan Zee. Carlsen has won there eight times but has not played since 2023.

Asked by Chess.com, he smiled: "It means that I'm not playing next year." He said he has a great relationship with the Dutch organizers and added that more coordination in chess would be good. "But it is what it is."

His schedule already carries plenty of speed events, and this is another one. Anyone who has followed his [Total Chess rating lead](/articles/total-chess-rating-puts-carlsen-at-2830-51-points-clear/) knows he stays active at fast time limits even while skipping the classical circuit.

**The $20 million question**

At the October 20 press conference, GM Miguel Illescas, an eight-time Spanish champion who worked with Vladimir Kramnik, asked whether Carlsen would return to the World Championship cycle for $20 million. "Most people have a price, obviously," Carlsen said, declining to name his.

He added that "there are a lot of things in chess that interest me more than a classical match." The seven remaining names, due in the coming weeks, will show how much of the top tier follows him to Barcelona rather than Wijk aan Zee.

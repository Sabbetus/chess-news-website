September 2026 brings 13 tracked tournaments across the African continent, a spread that runs from Cairo's rapid scene to a spring weekend in the Eastern Cape. Most of these events are rapid, one-day affairs built around clubs and academies rather than big federation opens, and the numbers reflect that: this is a month of local turnout more than marquee fields.

Egypt supplies the biggest draw. The [1st Engineers Syndicate Sharkia U20](https://chesstournamentcalendar.com/tournament/1st-engineers-syndicate-sharkia-u20-1481696/) runs September 11 in Sharqia and has pulled in 83 registered players, the largest field in this data set. Cairo's rapid circuit also shows up twice through Port Said: the [Traveller Tweet - Rapid Port Said (U12, U18)](https://chesstournamentcalendar.com/tournament/traveller-tweet-rapid-port-said-u18-1484475/) and the [Traveller Tweet - Rapid Port Said Open 2026](https://chesstournamentcalendar.com/tournament/traveller-tweet-rapid-port-said-open-2026-1484441/), both on September 7, with 45 and 37 players respectively.

Algeria's Bejaia region hosts a pair of same-name, same-day events on September 12. The larger [Journée Nationale d'El Moudjahid (Bejaia 2026)](https://chesstournamentcalendar.com/tournament/journ%C3%A9e-nationale-del-moudjahid-bejaia-2026-1491080/) has 57 registered so far, while a second listing under the identical title has drawn 33. Both are organized by Ni3ma Academy and Bougie Club Echecs at the same venue, suggesting parallel sections of one national commemorative day rather than separate tournaments.

South Africa contributes two classical weekends worth watching. The [Emalahleni Spring Open 2026](https://chesstournamentcalendar.com/tournament/emalahleni-spring-open-2026-1465653/) runs September 19 to 20 at Witbank High School with 40 players registered, and the [Rhodes Open 2026](https://chesstournamentcalendar.com/tournament/rhodes-open-2026-1484460/) follows the previous weekend, September 12 to 13 at Rhodes University, with 31 signed up. Both stand out as rare classical time-control entries in a month otherwise dominated by rapid formats.

Nigeria's [1st Kada City & Cyntoco U25 Chess Tournament, 2026](https://chesstournamentcalendar.com/tournament/1st-kada-city-cyntoco-u25-chess-tournament-2026-1473921/) runs September 11 to 12 in Kaduna with 53 players registered, backed by a joint organizing effort from the Kada Chess Club, Cyntoco Services and the Kaduna Chess Association. Later in the month, Morocco's [8eme Trophée National Feu Dr Abderrahmane Nejjar](https://chesstournamentcalendar.com/tournament/8eme-troph%C3%A9e-national-feu-dr-abderrahmane-nejjar-d-1487427/) takes place September 27 in Salé, a memorial event now in its eighth edition with 25 players registered.

Prize funds and rating requirements aren't tracked for any of these listings, so fields are best compared by registration numbers alone. Smaller entries round out the month too, including Kenya's [BACK TO SCHOOL TOURNAMENT](https://chesstournamentcalendar.com/tournament/back-to-school-tournament-1482890/) in Nairobi and South Africa's [Forever Knights Spring Rapid '26](https://chesstournamentcalendar.com/tournament/forever-knights-spring-rapid-26-1479400/) in Nxuba, both proof that September's calendar leans heavily on community-level chess rather than headline opens.

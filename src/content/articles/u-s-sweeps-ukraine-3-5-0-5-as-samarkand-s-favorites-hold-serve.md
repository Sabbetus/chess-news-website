Round four of the 46th Chess Olympiad in Samarkand played out about as calmly as a round can when 21 teams are still carrying perfect scores into it. On the top boards, where those unbeaten squads faced each other, [the favorites came through without a single upset](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-samarkand-chess-olympiad-round-4), a sharp contrast to the chaos of the earlier rounds in this record-sized event.

The loudest result of the day belonged to the United States, who beat Ukraine 3.5-0.5 in the open section. That scoreline was the most one-sided result among the top pairings, and it came against a Ukrainian team that had itself been sitting among the unbeaten group before round four.

GM Levon Aronian supplied the highlight, with his win singled out as the Game of the Day. For a player who has spent much of his career as one of the sport's most reliable Olympiad performers, delivering the decisive individual result in a top-board sweep is exactly the kind of form the U.S. team needs if it wants to stay in the perfect-score group deep into the event.

The U.S. didn't stop there. In the women's section, the American team posted the identical 3.5-0.5 scoreline, meaning the country swept both of its top-board matches on the same day. Two matching lopsided wins on the same round is a rare kind of symmetry, and it puts the U.S. among the more convincing performers of the day across both sections.

What makes round four notable isn't any single result so much as the absence of drama on the boards that mattered most. With 21 teams still perfect heading in, a top-11 pairing carries real weight: any loss there immediately reshapes the contenders for gold. That none of the favorites slipped up marks a break from the pattern that's defined Samarkand so far.

Earlier rounds had already produced their share of shocks, from [Argentina's win over Ukraine as a 28th seed](/articles/argentina-s-28th-seed-shocks-ukraine-at-samarkand/) to [El Salvador's stunning upset of Cuba](/articles/el-salvador-s-94th-seed-stuns-cuba-at-samarkand/) and [India losing Erigaisi on board one while its bench carried the team through](/articles/erigaisi-falls-but-india-s-bench-holds-at-samarkand/). Those results had already thinned the psychological safety net for any team assuming a smooth ride at the top of the standings.

Round four's calm doesn't mean the standings are settled. With this many teams still unbeaten, the pairings only get harder from here, and a top-11 group that stayed clean this round will not stay that large for long. The next round will start peeling names off that list, and how the U.S. handles being one of the teams left standing is worth watching heading into the middle rounds.

The reigning world champion goes into the Salesforce FIDE World Championship Match 2026 in Geneva with an uncomfortable number next to his name. According to [FIDE's own preview](https://en.chessbase.com/post/world-championship-2026-gukesh-pelletier), published by ChessBase, Gukesh Dommaraju has slipped to 26th place on the rating list, on 2710 Elo points.

That is a long way from where the 2024 Budapest Olympiad left him. There he scored 9/10 on board one with a rating performance of 3056, which pushed him into the world's top five and to a new personal peak.

**A slide that began mid-2025**

FIDE dates the downturn to around the middle of 2025, with results described as increasingly inconsistent and a series of disappointing performances. The press release frames it as the first significant plateau of his career.

You can see where he now sits on The Chess Herald's [FIDE Top 100](/rankings/) table, and the women's list is at the [FIDE Women's Top 25](/rankings/women/) for comparison with the rest of the rating picture. For a champion, 26th is unusual company. The rating line also tells you how much of his standing rests on the 2024 title rather than on current form.

**The challenger's numbers**

His opponent arrives from the other direction. Javokhir Sindarov won the 2025 World Cup and then took the Candidates Tournament in April 2026, which FIDE describes as dominating it. The gap between them on paper was already measured in our look at how [Sindarov leads Gukesh by 72 points on the November FIDE list](/articles/sindarov-leads-gukesh-by-72-points-on-the-november-fide-list/).

A 72-point gap is not trivial. Under the [Elo system](/articles/chess-ratings/), a difference of that size implies the higher-rated player is expected to score clearly more than half the points, though a short classical match leaves plenty of room for variance.

**What Gukesh still has**

The press release lists his strengths as opening versatility, exceptional calculating ability, resilience under pressure and a fearless approach to complicated positions. The evidence it cites is his record in big moments: winning the Candidates as the youngest ever, and the fourteenth and final game in Singapore.

That last point matters. He lost game one against Ding Liren with White, then won the match 7½-6½ after Ding erred in a dramatic endgame. Rating lists do not capture that sort of late-match nerve, and it is the best argument that 2710 understates him in a head-to-head fight.

**Reflection before the match**

FIDE also notes that Gukesh reportedly sought time for reflection and preparation, and that his visit to the Tirumala Temple in Andhra Pradesh in early November drew attention.

He is the youngest undisputed champion in history, having broken a record Garry Kasparov held for more than four decades. At 26th, he also carries the reverse distinction of a champion trailing most of the elite he is supposed to lead.

Peter Leko and Peter Svidler are set to commentate the match, as covered in our note on [Leko and Svidler getting the mic](/articles/leko-and-svidler-get-the-mic-for-gukesh-v-sindarov-in-geneva/).

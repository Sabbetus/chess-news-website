The 2026 World Chess Championship match between Gukesh Dommaraju and Javokhir Sindarov starts today in Geneva, with games scheduled from November 24 to December 12. Ahead of the match, five-time World Champion Viswanathan Anand gave Chess.com an interview, conducted by CM Tickoo Sahil, in which he [put the two players at roughly 50-50](https://www.chess.com/news/view/vishy-anand-gukesh-sindarov-world-championship-predictions).

His reasoning is less about numbers than about state of mind. For a reader who has followed the build-up, including [Kasparov's lean toward Gukesh and the Nakamura and Carlsen picks for Sindarov](/articles/kasparov-leans-gukesh-nakamura-and-carlsen-pick-sindarov/), Anand offers a view from someone who has sat on both sides of this kind of match.

**Why he backed Sindarov early**

Anand says he rarely makes predictions and tends to "waffle", but this time he picked Sindarov for the Candidates, alongside Fabiano Caruana: "one old horse and one young horse." What swayed him was Sindarov's attitude to danger at the GCL in Mumbai last year, where the two were teammates and Anand saw it up close.

He did not foresee the margin. Anand says Sindarov "caught Fabi in preparation, he caught Hikaru," beat everyone round after round, and even spoiled a couple of winning positions, so the score could have been higher. Anish Giri finished second and Caruana third.

**A "Zen" challenger**

The danger of Sindarov, in Anand's view, is simply "that it can happen": he can show up, play at a very high level and be in great form. He notes the Uzbek has been stable all year, recovering from the odd setback on the Grand Chess Tour and playing well against top players.

Anand also pointed to his demeanor: Sindarov travels, watches football and seems to be enjoying himself. "That's a Zen kind of state to be in," he said. For more on how the challenger got here, see [how Sindarov went from slow starter to title challenger](/articles/how-sindarov-went-from-slow-starter-to-world-title-challenger/).

**What Gukesh carries from Ding**

Asked about experience, Anand said "the difference between one and zero is very high." Gukesh faced almost everything in his match with Ding Liren: he led, he trailed. "I would rather have played one than zero," Anand said.

He cautioned that form is mostly attitude and is hard to fake. Gukesh's recent struggles, he suggested, come from handling the pressure and responsibility of being champion, perhaps with misaligned expectations. Gukesh has himself [admitted he hasn't handled the pressure of the title](/articles/gukesh-admits-he-hasn-t-handled-the-pressure-of-the-title/).

**Is form a guide?**

Anand argued that form can simply indicate strength, and that we wrongly treat everything as momentum. Yet he warned that both Sindarov's and Gukesh's current form can end. Last time, he noted, Ding was not in good shape and was frustrated with life, but still played a match with big swings.

**First blood**

The most vivid part of the interview concerns the first decisive game. Anand says that until Geneva players are in a "dream-like state," and that after the first result "everybody wakes up", both teams and the public.

For the leader, the aim is to play exactly as before with something in the bank. For the player behind, he described a gradual, calm push with no silly moves, and said being behind has freed him in the few times it happened. An early result, he added, is a test of endurance: you may have to hold that attitude for 10 to 20 days.

**Preparation and youth**

Both teams, Anand said, will prepare surprises and struggle to predict the other side. For Gukesh's camp the priority is that he feels good after every game, thinking he can do even better.

For Sindarov's, it is how to maintain and build on his form. Both players are about 20, and energy shouldn't be a problem, but "a match is draining even for 20-year-olds." His verdict: "all things equal, I'd rather be 20!"

Asked for a percentage for Gukesh, Anand said they have comparable strength, though not comparable form, so roughly 50-50. He added that it doesn't matter much and that he is more curious who gets the first win. Game 1 is today.

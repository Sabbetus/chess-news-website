Istanbul will host the 2026 FIDE World Rapid and Blitz Championships from December 26 to 30, [FIDE announced on Thursday](https://www.chess.com/news/view/istanbul-to-host-2026-world-rapid-blitz-championships). The event crowns up to four world champions, across the Open and Women's sections in both rapid and blitz.

Players had been waiting for the host city to be confirmed, with less than two months to go. The venue, tournament regulations and registration details are still to be published. [FIDE's own announcement](https://www.fide.com/istanbul-to-host-fide-world-rapid-and-blitz-championships-2026/) says arrivals are on December 25 and that the information will come "shortly".

**The defending champions**

Magnus Carlsen arrives holding both Open titles. In Doha last year he completed his fifth rapid and blitz double.

He won his sixth World Rapid title on 10.5/13, a full point clear of the field. In the blitz final he beat GM Nodirbek Abdusattorov 2.5-1.5 for his ninth title in that discipline.

In the Women's section the titles were split. GM Aleksandra Goryachkina won her first Women's World Rapid title, beating GM Zhu Jiner in a playoff.

GM Bibisara Assaubayeva won the Women's World Blitz for the third time. She beat GM Anna Muzychuk 2.5-1.5 in the final.

**Format and sponsor**

No regulations are out yet, but last year's pattern is a fair guide. The rapid was a Swiss tournament, while the blitz began with a Swiss stage and finished with four-player knockouts. For readers new to the pairing system, our [Swiss explainer](/articles/swiss-system-tiebreaks/) covers how it works.

Freedom Holding Corp., FIDE's strategic partner since 2023, is the title sponsor. FIDE says its 2026 agreement also covers the Candidates and the World Championship match, plus training centres, school programmes and talent scouting.

The financial group is led by FIDE President Timur Turlov, who was elected to head the federation in September. That makes the sponsor and the federation's president tightly linked.

Turlov said Türkiye "has repeatedly demonstrated its ability to organise world-class chess events." He thanked the Turkish Chess Federation and its partners, and spoke of celebrating the end of the chess year in "one of the world's most dynamic and welcoming cities."

**Why Istanbul**

The city has done this before at the top level. It staged the 2012 Chess Olympiad, 14 years ago, which FIDE counts as the 40th.

Türkiye also hosted the World U9-U17 Rapid & Blitz Championships in Antalya in December 2025.
FIDE also points to Istanbul's role as a transport hub linking Europe, Asia, Africa and the Americas. For a field of hundreds of players arriving in the last week of December, easy flights are a real practical argument.

There is a home-interest angle too. GMs Yagiz Kaan Erdogmus and Ediz Gurel, two of the country's leading young players, get a chance to face the world's best in front of a home crowd.

Whether the rest of the elite follows Carlsen is not yet known, since the field depends on regulations and registration that have not been published.

Uzbekistan go into the last round of the Samarkand Olympiad with the gold medal in their own hands. The hosts [beat unbeaten Armenia 3½-½ in round ten](https://en.chessbase.com/post/chess-olympiad-2026-10) to reach 18/20 match points, one ahead of India on 17.

Germany, Ukraine and Hungary sit on 16, and those five are the only teams that can still finish first. Eight teams share 15/20, including the Netherlands, so they are out of the title race.

**Armenia broken on all fronts**

Armenia had lost only two individual games in nine rounds, and both came in draws against higher-seeded opponents, the United States and the Netherlands. Uzbekistan cracked them far more decisively.

Javokhir Sindarov and Mukhiddin Madaminov won relatively quickly after their opponents went wrong tactically in double-edged middlegames. Nodirbek Abdusattorov added the third point, though he needed patience against Haik Martirosyan, eventually [converting a rook endgame with opposite-coloured bishops](#game-embed).

Abdusattorov is the only Uzbek to have played all ten rounds. He has 8/10 and a 2900 performance on board one. Sindarov is second on board two behind Jorden van Foreest, and Nodirbek Yakubboev is second on board three behind Germany's Frederik Svane.

**India stay within reach**

India, the defending champions, beat Uzbekistan 2 by the same 3½-½ score. They had rating edges of more than 200 Elo on three boards. Praggnanandhaa, Nihal Sarin and Gukesh won, and Arjun Erigaisi drew with Abdimalik Abdisalimov.

Gukesh has 8½/10 and has played every round, with a 2797 performance that makes him the clear favourite for board-four gold. In round nine he beat Alexander Donchenko, the only decisive game in India's 2½-1½ win over Germany. Praggnanandhaa is unbeaten on board one with 6/9.

**Ukraine's first upset**

Ukraine kept their hopes alive by beating fourth-seeded China 3-1, their first upset match win of the event. Igor Kovalenko beat former world champion Ding Liren on board one. Roman Dehtiarov, the 18-year-old European champion, beat Kong Xiangrui on board four.

Vasyl Ivanchuk drew with Wei Yi and Ihor Samunenkov drew with Xu Xiangyu. The 17-year-old Samunenkov has 7½/9 on board four, second on that board behind only Gukesh.

**Germany and Hungary hold on**

Germany beat France 3-1. Vincent Keymer and Matthias Bluebaum drew on the top two boards, while brothers Frederik and Rasmus Svane won on boards three and four. The same pattern had worked against Türkiye and earlier against Uzbekistan. Frederik leads board three with 8/10 and a 2819 performance.

Hungary beat Spain 3-1 in a match where all four games were decisive. Richard Rapport, Peter Leko and Gleb Dudin won, while Jaime Santos Latasa beat Benjamin Gledura for Spain. Dudin, the reserve, has 7/9 and four straight wins.

**The final round**

Uzbekistan face Ukraine, who can catch them on match points with a win. India play Hungary, and Germany meet the Netherlands. For India, anything short of a win would make it very hard to overhaul Uzbekistan.

The round starts four hours earlier than usual, at 8.00 CEST. The Uzbek side's run is already covered in [our look inside the gold-medal squad](/articles/the-anti-motivator-and-the-silent-killer-inside-uzbekistan-s-gold-medal-team/), and on the board they need one more strong result at home.

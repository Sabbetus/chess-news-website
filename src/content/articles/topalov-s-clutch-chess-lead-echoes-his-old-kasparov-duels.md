Garry Kasparov has spent much of his post-retirement career being the one who makes other legends sweat. On the first day of the 2026 Clutch Chess: The Legends, it was the other way around. Veselin Topalov [took a 2.5-1.5 lead after Kasparov struggled to manage his clock and his composure](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-clutch-chess-legends-kasparov-topalov-day-1), with Topalov making the most of the chances that came his way.

That description alone tells you something about how the day went. Clutch Chess events are built around must-win pressure by design, and a 13th World Champion fighting his own clock as much as his opponent is exactly the kind of storyline the format is meant to produce.

It also isn't the first time these two have given each other trouble. Kasparov and Topalov faced each other periodically through the 1990s and early 2000s, a stretch when Topalov was rising into the world's elite while Kasparov sat at the very top of it.

Their most famous meeting came at Linares in 1999, a game that still gets replayed and dissected decades later as one of the sharpest attacking efforts of Kasparov's career.

That history is part of why this rematch carries weight beyond nostalgia. Chessori already looked at the pairing when the current series began, in a piece on [why Kasparov vs. Topalov in Chess960 feels like unfinished business](/articles/why-kasparov-vs-topalov-in-chess960-feels-like-unfinished-business/). A day where Topalov leads 2.5-1.5 fits that framing well: two players whose old games still get studied, now generating a new result under modern clocks and modern time pressure.

There's an irony in how this particular day unfolded. Kasparov built his reputation partly on ruthless practical play and keeping opponents on the back foot with the clock as a weapon. Watching him be the one undone by time trouble against Topalov, of all opponents, is the kind of role reversal that legends events are almost designed to surface.

None of that changes what the scoreboard says after Day 1. Topalov leads 2.5-1.5, and however this particular match started, the standings are what both players carry into the next stage of Clutch Chess: The Legends. Whether Kasparov can claw the deficit back, or whether Topalov extends it, is the open question heading into the next day of play.

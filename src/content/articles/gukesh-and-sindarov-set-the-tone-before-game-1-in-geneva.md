The 2026 Salesforce FIDE World Championship opens in Geneva with a press conference and ceremony, and [Sindarov will have White in Game 1](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-fide-chess-world-championship-opening-ceremony) on Tuesday, 24 November, at 15:00 CET.

Gukesh Dommaraju, the reigning champion, picked a clock holding a black chocolate knight at the opening ceremony in the Palace of Nations. That left the white knight, and the first move, to Javokhir Sindarov.

It was a quieter way to settle colours than the laser display used in Singapore in 2024, when Gukesh played Ding Liren. Here the choice was between light and dark chocolate knights, which fits the Swiss setting.

**Two different tones**

Gukesh set the mood early. Asked whether defending a title is harder, he answered: "I don't think I'm here to defend anything—I'm here to win it all over again!"

Given how the last couple of years have gone for him, the line reads as a deliberate reset. Elsewhere in the press conference he said that whatever has happened until now "just gets erased when we sit at the table for game one."

Sindarov sounded more like a man enjoying the ride. He said the six months since winning the Candidates went quickly, and that "many chess players play their whole life in chess and they don't have a chance to play for this." He put his chances at 50:50, and added that in a 14-game match "anything can happen."

**Why Sindarov played so much**

Sindarov was asked why he played so many events before the match. He said he already had a Grand Chess Tour invitation before the Candidates, and that his aim afterwards was to gain experience, since he hadn't played many elite tournaments. Tata Steel was among them, and he noted he played against Gukesh there.

Gukesh said he wasn't surprised. His team expected Sindarov to play more, and they had chosen to slow down this year.

His reading was that Sindarov was using good momentum against the top players. For background on how the challenger got here, see [how Sindarov went from slow starter to world title challenger](/articles/how-sindarov-went-from-slow-starter-to-world-title-challenger/).

**Seconds, food and engines**

Neither player revealed a full team. Gukesh named Gaju and promised other names later. Sindarov listed coach Roman Vidonyak, Mukhiddin Madaminov and Nikita Vitiugov, and said he has other helpers he will show after the match.

On food, Gukesh is getting home-cooked Indian meals from people in Geneva. Sindarov said he eats any cuisine. He also recalled his October training camp in Cyprus, where he met Hikaru Nakamura.

On engines, Sindarov said they have changed a lot in the last six years, opening preparation is very different from matches ten years ago, and long lines can feel boring to prepare. Gukesh argued computers raise everyone's level and make wins harder, but also "makes it more of a sport," since everyone has similar tools and has to find a small edge.

**The match setup**

The match is 14 classical games at the Fondation Martin Bodmer in Cologny, overlooking Lake Geneva, with a $2.5 million prize fund. Games begin daily at 3 p.m. local time.

A player who reaches 7½ points ends the match early. Tiebreaks, if needed, are on 12 December. The [World Chess Championship guide](/events/world-chess-championship/#how-it-works) covers the format.

Gukesh said he checked the hall the day before: the chairs, lighting and floors. He saw the many books around the players but didn't check which ones they are. Peter Leko and Peter Svidler are the official commentators on FIDE's YouTube channel.

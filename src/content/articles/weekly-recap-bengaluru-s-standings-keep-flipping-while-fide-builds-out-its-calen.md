Global Chess League Season 4 supplied most of the week's drama, with the standings changing hands almost every night in Bengaluru. Around it, FIDE kept adding to its institutional calendar and the rating list moved in some notable ways.

Magnus Carlsen and Ian Nepomniachtchi were set to share a board again, [this time with nothing like a world title on the line](/articles/carlsen-and-nepomniachtchi-meet-again-this-time-with-nothing-like-a-world-title/). Their last meeting with real stakes was the 2021 world championship match, which Carlsen won 7.5-3.5.

Day one belonged to someone else entirely. Javokhir Sindarov [beat Viswanathan Anand and held Alireza Firouzja to a draw](/articles/the-torch-passes-again-sindarov-s-win-over-anand-echoes-a-familiar-chess-story/), powering the FYERS American Gambits to a clean sweep on opening night. Carlsen made his own return to the league that same day: his Alpine APL Pipers [beat CheQ Mumba Masters 10-8](/articles/carlsen-returns-but-gcl-day-one-belongs-to-sindarov/), a result made stranger by the fact that the Pipers won the whole tournament last season without him.

The Carlsen-Nepomniachtchi match itself arrived a day later, and Carlsen needed to dig himself out of trouble to keep his team unbeaten. [His escape extended a pattern that's followed the two men for years](/articles/carlsen-s-escape-extends-old-nepomniachtchi-pattern/): Nepomniachtchi is the opponent who has given him the most trouble of anyone in the last five seasons.

By day three the table had already been turned upside down again. [Alpine APL Pipers seized the lead despite losing one of their two matches that day](/articles/gcl-s-wild-day-3-gambits-fall-pipers-seize-the-lead/), a reminder of how volatile the franchise format can be.

Day four brought the strangest story of the week. FYERS American Gambits reclaimed top spot and Triveni Continental Kings upset the overnight leaders, but [the match everyone kept talking about turned on a single second on a chess clock](/articles/a-second-s-difference-gcl-s-prodigy-board-controversy/).

The lead swung back again on day five. FYERS American Gambits [beat Triveni Continental Kings 8-5 to reclaim first place](/articles/gcl-s-lead-swings-back-to-the-gambits-on-day-5/), overtaking Alpine APL Pipers, who had briefly led earlier that same day after their own win.

Away from Bengaluru, the September FIDE rating list threw up its own surprise. Wesley So returned to the world top 5 and Praggnanandhaa Rameshbabu re-entered the top 10, but [the number worth sitting with belonged to Deekshitha, further down the list](/articles/deekshithas-record-gain-shows-how-fast-the-fide-rating-list-can-move/).

South America's August tournament calendar had its own hierarchy. Across 801 tracked events, [Brazil's grassroots rapid scene claimed most of the biggest fields](/articles/brazil-dominates-south-america-s-biggest-august-fields/), with Colombia and Argentina picking up notable entries of their own. Africa told a similar story from a different country: [South Africa supplied most of the continent's largest August fields](/articles/south-africa-dominates-africa-s-august-chess-rankings/), though the single biggest turnout came from a women's championship on the Western Cape coast.

Nigel Short is closing out a decades-long personal project. He's set to [travel to Antarctica and the Geographic South Pole for the Polar Chess Expedition](/articles/nigel-short-s-last-continent-is-antarctica/), a FIDE-rated rapid event billed as the most geographically extreme tournament ever staged.

FIDE also opened the door for players to have a bigger say in how the federation runs itself. It's [taking candidates for its new Athletes Commission](/articles/fide-s-new-athletes-commission-echoes-the-ioc-s-playbook/), which will have five to nine members with mandatory representation of both genders.

The federation is folding another standalone event into Olympiad season, too. The [FIDE Summit on Chess in Education will run in Samarkand on 24 September](/articles/fide-s-education-summit-follows-a-familiar-institutional-playbook/), alongside the Olympiad and its Congress, as the centerpiece of the 2026 Year of Chess in Education.

On the sponsorship side, FIDE landed a partner most federations spend years chasing. [Salesforce signed a multi-year deal](/articles/salesforce-s-fide-deal-is-a-software-sale-with-a-chessboard-attached/) making it Title Sponsor of the World Championship Matches through 2026 and 2028, the 2027 Women's Match, and the official AI partner behind the FIDE Rankings.

At the board, Veselin Topalov opened well against an old rival. He [took a 2.5-1.5 lead over Garry Kasparov on the first day of Clutch Chess: The Legends](/articles/topalov-s-clutch-chess-lead-echoes-his-old-kasparov-duels/), with Kasparov struggling to manage his clock and composure.

Uzbekistan added a genuine first to its growing hosting résumé this week, too, tying [ancient ivory chess pieces to a modern Samarkand milestone](/articles/samarkand-links-ancient-ivory-pieces-to-a-modern-milestone/). Samarkand hosted a second Olympiad of its own this week as well: the [third FIDE Chess Olympiad for People with Disabilities opened with 41 teams beginning play in Round 1](/articles/samarkand-s-disability-olympiad-echoes-belgrade-s-first-steps/), with the ceremonial first move made on Board 1 of Poland against Mongolia.

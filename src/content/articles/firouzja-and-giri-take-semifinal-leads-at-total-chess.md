On the first day of the semifinals of the Total Chess World Championship Tour Pilot in Budapest, [Alireza Firouzja beat Fabiano Caruana 12-8](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-total-chess-world-championship-tour-pilot-day-7) and Anish Giri beat Maxime Vachier-Lagrave 14.5-5.5. Both winners now need only a drawn day on Wednesday to reach the final. Their opponents must win outright to force an Armageddon game.

**How the day is scored**

Each knockout day has four blitz games at 3+2, worth one point per win. Then come two rapid games at 15+10, worth four each, and a single Fast Classic game at 45+30, worth eight.

Draws score half. One Fast Classic win therefore outweighs a clean blitz sweep.

The score resets overnight. If the trailing player wins Wednesday, the match goes to Armageddon, where whoever has more points over the two days picks the color. The semifinal winners meet in the final on Thursday and Friday.

**Firouzja holds on**

Firouzja swept the blitz 4-0. Caruana said afterward, "The blitz was really awful, I was just blundering everything every game." In the second blitz game he could have won on the spot with 34.Rg1!, threatening mate on g7 and the black queen, but took on b4 instead.

Caruana wiped out the deficit at once, winning the first rapid game with Black to make it 4-4. In the second rapid game he built a near-winning position with White, but Firouzja turned it around with [a dramatic attack](#game-embed). That game was Chess.com's Game of the Day, and it put Firouzja 8-4 up.

Caruana then had to win the Fast Classic game on demand. Of nine players in that spot on the day, only two managed it.

Firouzja admitted the game "got out of hand, out of my control," but found a narrow path to safety. He was even winning at one point before taking a draw that clinched the day 12-8.

**Giri's good blitz day**

Giri won the blitz 2.5-1.5 against the top seed. Three games were drawn, two of them after Giri missed wins, and he took the other when Vachier-Lagrave grabbed a pawn.

"On a good day I can play well! I'm much less stable than Maxime, but I have days when I play like a world-class blitz player," he said.

Both rapid games were drawn, leaving Giri 6.5-5.5 up. In the Fast Classic game, Vachier-Lagrave said he "over-pushed," and Giri's hanging pawns turned from a weakness into a strength. The win took Giri up to world number eight.

Asked what he would carry into Wednesday, Vachier-Lagrave said, "Hopefully I don't remember anything I've done from today!"

**Carlsen skips the finale**

Magnus Carlsen scored 11.5/12 against Bibisara Assaubayeva in the bracket for places 17 to 20: 3.5/4 in blitz, then 8/8 in rapid. That clinched the day before the last game, and he did not show up for it. Assaubayeva waited at the board until she was awarded the forfeit, making the final score 11.5-8.5.

The game carried more weight than the match itself. A draw would have dropped Carlsen below 2800 for the first time in 17 years, according to Chess.com.

His rating and the world number-one spot were on the line.

**Elsewhere in the bracket**

Hans Niemann beat Le Quang Liem 13.5-6.5 after winning on demand in the Fast Classic game. Ian Nepomniachtchi beat Jan-Krzysztof Duda 15-5, joking about "Tilted Tuesday."

Haik Martirosyan trailed Richard Rapport 3-9 before the Fast Classic game, then won it with Black as Rapport collapsed and resigned on move 26. That gave Martirosyan the day 11-9. Wesley So beat Vincent Keymer 14-6, and Vladislav Artemiev beat Andrew Hong 17-3.

Players were openly critical of the format. Hikaru Nakamura called starting with blitz "ridiculous," and Jorden van Foreest said, "my head is just aching, just too much chess today." Total Chess says it will gather feedback before its first full season starts in March 2027.

Day eight starts at 9 a.m. ET / 15:00 CET, with Armageddon where needed. The finals and third-place matches follow on 19 and 20 November.

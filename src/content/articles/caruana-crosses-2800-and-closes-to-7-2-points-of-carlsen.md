Fabiano Caruana is now rated exactly 2800.0 on the live list, and Magnus Carlsen's grip on first place has loosened sharply. After day four of the Total Chess World Championship Tour Pilot 2026 in Budapest, [the gap between them is 7.2 points](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-total-chess-world-championship-tour-pilot-day-4). Carlsen started the event 40 points ahead.

On the live list, Caruana's win and draw on day four earned him 17 points, while Carlsen has dropped 15.8 in total. That is a 32.8-point swing in three days. You can follow the full ordering in our [FIDE Top 100](/rankings/) table.

**How Carlsen lost ground**

[Carlsen lost to Haik Martirosyan](#game-embed) of Armenia, rated 2669, in the first game of their day-four match. It was his first defeat of the event after five draws. He again spent too much time in the opening, and was 11 minutes behind on the clock when the position turned critical.

Martirosyan said he felt Carlsen's king was weak and his own safer, so he went for a win. He eventually wove a mating net.

"It's like a dream for me," he said. "I was just dreaming to play against him a classical game, but to win is already special."

Only half an hour later came the second game. Carlsen tried 8.g4!? after a seven-minute think, which looked a lot like tilt.

He survived and drew, which kept him at No. 1 for now. Martirosyan leads Group C, with Anish Giri a point behind after beating Ian Nepomniachtchi.

**Caruana's side of it**

Caruana beat Vincent Keymer in the first game, [his third straight win over an elite rival](/articles/caruana-leads-group-b-on-10-points-after-sweeping-praggnanandhaa/). He converted a single easy-to-miss slip by Keymer, and the game was named Game of the Day. The rematch was a 72-move draw in which he admitted being "very close to losing."

That draw matters for the list. Caruana could potentially have overtaken Carlsen in that second round. He leads Group B, with Liem Le the closest pursuer after surviving two games against Richard Rapport.

Asked about catching Carlsen, Caruana was calm. "No, not too much, I have reached 2800 a few times before. If it gets a bit closer, I'll definitely think about it, but for the moment I'm not thinking about it too much."

**History and the K factor**

Carlsen has been world number one for more than 15 years without a break. The closest anyone came was around the 2018 World Championship match against Caruana, when the gap shrank to three points. He is also in danger of falling below 2800 for the first time in 17 years.

One reason the swings are so large is the time control. Levon Aronian suggested on social media that games under 1 hour 30 minutes should carry a K factor of 5 or 6.

He said it does not feel right to equate a 5-6 hour game with a 2.5 hour one. With a lower factor, equally rated opponents would earn three points for a win rather than five.

[Our explainer on ratings](/articles/chess-ratings/) covers how that factor drives movement.

Carlsen needs results, and not only for the Total Chess standings: he sits at the bottom of Group C and is winless. Rounds seven and eight are today, Saturday 14 November, from 15:00 CET, with Carlsen facing Mamedyarov and Caruana meeting Le.

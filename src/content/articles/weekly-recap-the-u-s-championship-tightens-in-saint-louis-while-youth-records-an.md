Eighteen stories this week, and the biggest share came from Saint Louis, where the U.S. Championships went from a two-man lead to a four-way tie and back to a single leader. Around that, a Chinese final went to Armageddon, an 11-year-old moved closer to a record, and two big youth and tournament-calendar stories rounded things out.

**The U.S. Championships**

The open section started the week with Wesley So and Awonder Liang [drawing in 30 moves without much action](/articles/woodward-and-shankland-win-as-so-and-liang-stay-level-at-2-5/) in round three. Both stayed on 2.5/3. Two decisive games behind them reshaped the chasing pack.

In round four, [Fabiano Caruana beat Ray Robson to join So in the lead](/articles/caruana-joins-so-in-the-lead-as-liang-falls-to-xiong/) on 3/4. Liang lost to Xiong that day. Alice Lee took the sole top spot in the women's event.

Caruana also won Titled Tuesday on the rest day, [finishing outright first on 9.5 points](/articles/caruana-wins-titled-tuesday-on-a-u-s-championship-rest-day/). It did not carry over to the board. In round five, [Andy Woodward beat the defending champion](/articles/woodward-beats-caruana-as-eight-of-12-games-are-decisive/) in the bloodiest round yet, with eight of 12 games decisive across both tournaments.

By the halfway point, [So, Woodward, Caruana and Liang shared the open lead](/articles/caruana-and-liang-join-a-four-way-lead-after-round-6/). In the Women's, Rachael Li beat leader Alice Lee, which left a three-way tie at the top.

A round later, [So beat Ray Robson to lead alone on 5/7](/articles/so-takes-sole-lead-on-5-7-as-liang-falls-in-27-moves/), half a point ahead of Caruana and Woodward, while Li and Lee share the women's lead on 5½/7.

**Finals, norms and upsets elsewhere**

Yu Yangyi won the Shenzhen Nanshan Masters by [beating Ding Liren in an Armageddon game with White](/articles/yu-yangyi-beats-ding-liren-in-armageddon-to-win-shenzhen/). The all-Chinese final could not be separated until the second tiebreak stage.

In Norway, Rinat Jumabayev [won Fagernes on tiebreak](/articles/jumabayev-wins-fagernes-as-gloeckler-secures-a-second-gm-norm/) with 7½/10, the only unbeaten player in the 62-player GM section. Tiger Hillarp Persson and G. B. Harshavardhan also reached 7½ but had weaker tiebreaks. Christian Gloeckler, 14, secured his second GM norm.

At the Atyrau Masters, [both top seeds lost within two rounds](/articles/atyrau-masters-two-seeds-fall-anand-and-ahmad-lead-on-3-3/). Nguyen Duc Hoa (2405) beat Shamsiddin Vokhidov (2649), and Sergey Sklokin-Bagiyan (2477) beat Leon Luke Mendonca (2629). Anand and Ahmad lead on 3/3.

The European Club Cup opened in Budva, where [the favourites swept round one but Nils Grandelius lost to a 55-year-old club player](/articles/favourites-sweep-as-grandelius-falls-on-day-one-of-the-european-club-cup/).

In Germany, [Viernheim lead Baden-Baden on board points](/articles/viernheim-lead-baden-baden-on-board-points-after-three-bundesliga-rounds/) after three Bundesliga rounds. Both clubs took 6 match points, but Viernheim have 17½ board points, four more than their rivals.

**Young players**

IM Roman Shogdzhiev, 11, [scored his second GM norm](/articles/roman-shogdzhiev-11-is-one-gm-norm-from-mishra-s-record/) in the Serbian Super League. He needs one more norm and 29 rating points to pass Abhimanyu Mishra's 2021 mark as the youngest grandmaster ever.

The 2026 FIDE World Championships U7-U17 [opened in Roquetas de Mar](/articles/fast-classical-debuts-at-a-721-player-youth-worlds-in-spain/) with 721 players from 82 countries. It runs until October 21 at the Evenia Zoraida Beach Resort.

In the Under-13 Open, Egypt's Abdalrahman Sameh Mohamed [has won all six games](/articles/sameh-meets-top-seed-kuandykuly-after-a-6-6-start-at-the-world-u13/). After the rest day he meets Danis Kuandykuly of Kazakhstan, the rating favourite, who has dropped only one draw.

**History and the calendar**

Munich 1936 was an event the Greater German Chess Federation called an Olympiad, though FIDE never gave it that status. We looked back at [the tournament FIDE never recognised](/articles/munich-1936-the-unofficial-olympiad-hungary-won-20-0/), which ran from 17 August to 1 September alongside the Olympic Games.

Rafael Vaganian was born in Yerevan on 15 October 1951, and [turns 75 this week](/articles/rafael-vaganian-at-75-the-armenian-who-reached-no-4/). His career covers the Soviet school, the post-Soviet years and a long stretch in German club chess.

For planners, European organizers have 330 tournaments on the November calendar, and [199 seniors are heading to Usedom for nine days](/articles/europe-in-november-199-players-head-to-usedom-for-nine-days/). In Asia, 168 tournaments are tracked, and [Bengbu's 198-player classical tops the list](/articles/bengbu-s-198-player-classical-tops-asia-s-november-calendar/).

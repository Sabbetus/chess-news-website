Game 3 of the Salesforce FIDE World Championship 2026 in Geneva [starts today, Thursday, November 26, at 9 a.m. ET](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-fide-world-chess-championship-game-3), which is 15:00 CET and 7:30 p.m. IST. Challenger Javokhir Sindarov has the white pieces against defending champion Gukesh Dommaraju. The match is level at 1-1 after two draws.

It is the last game before the players get their first rest day, so both have a reason to want something from it. A decisive result would send one of them into the break ahead, and a third draw would send them in level.

**Where the match stands**

Sindarov is rated 2782 and Gukesh 2710, a gap of 72 points. Under the [rating logic](/articles/chess-ratings/) most people use, that makes the challenger the favorite, though neither game so far has shown it on the board.

Game 1 was a draw in which Gukesh survived some pressure. Game 2 was a quick one: a 20-move London System draw that ended abruptly.

That short draw is the interesting data point. Whether Gukesh is happy to keep steering toward quiet positions, or whether Sindarov will try to push harder with the first move now, is the question Game 3 should begin to answer.

**Format and stakes**

The match is 14 games, and the first player to reach 7.5 points wins. The time control is two hours for 40 moves, then 30 minutes to the end of the game, with 30 seconds added per move from move 41.

The prize fund is $2,500,000, with $200,000 going to the winner of the match and the rest split equally. If the match is tied 7-7, a playoff follows, starting with four games of 15+10 rapid chess.

With only 11 games left after today's, there is no shortage of time. But the match's structure punishes slow starts less than a short event would, which may explain why both players have been content to bank draws early.

Anand called the match 50-50 before the start and was waiting for first blood. Two games in, nobody has drawn it.

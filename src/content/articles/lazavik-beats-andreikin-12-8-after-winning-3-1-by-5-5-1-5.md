In the Round of 16 of the Speed Chess Championship XI, GM Denis Lazavik [beat GM Dmitry Andreikin 12-8](https://www.chess.com/news/view/2026-speed-chess-championship-r16-lazavik-andreikin) and moved into the Quarterfinals. He will face the winner of GM Wesley So against GM Praggnanandhaa Rameshbabu. Lazavik earned $6,500 and Andreikin $1,625 by win percentage.

The match was far closer in play than in score for long stretches. Andreikin kept getting winning chances and kept missing them, and the frustration showed as the match went on.

**5+1: Lazavik 4-3**

Lazavik won the first game, where a bishop sacrifice could not be accepted after one fatal move by Andreikin. Commentator GM Robert Hess had just praised both players' maneuvering as "Karpovian."

Andreikin nearly won a rook endgame in game two, which was drawn. Game three was the wildest of the match. Andreikin broke through with a winning attack but could not find the knockout, and Lazavik then took a draw by repetition in a position where he had a simple winning move that was easy to miss.

Games two to seven, the rest of the 5+1 segment, were all drawn. Lazavik was candid about it: "when I don't play well I just try to make draws and usually, if you don't lose, you'll win at some point."

**3+1: Lazavik 5.5-1.5**

Lazavik won the first 3+1 game with Black, building pressure until White's position fell apart. Two quiet draws followed. Then Andreikin briefly had a winning pawn grab in the third game and did not convert.

After 11 games Lazavik led by only two points. He then won four straight. The first two ran 72 and 87 moves, and Andreikin came painfully close to saving both.

In the last 3+1 game Andreikin opened [1.h4](#ply-1), got a decent position, and watched it crumble fast. Lazavik's view was that Andreikin had noticed how long the unbeaten run was and that it got to him. "when he played [1.h4](#ply-1), he started tilting," he said.

**1+1: Andreikin 3.5-2.5**

Lazavik won the first bullet game, the third segment in a row he opened with a win. Andreikin answered in game 16, his first win of the match. It lasted 133 moves and ended with a mating net just as Lazavik seemed to have escaped again.

That cut the gap to five points. Andreikin missed excellent chances in the next game, and the match was realistically over. He took the bullet section, but only as a consolation.

**What it means**

Both players started the match third and fourth on the Champions Chess Tour leaderboard, which decides 12 qualifiers for the 2027 Esports World Cup. Lazavik's pedigree in fast chess is clear: third in the Speed Chess Championship in London earlier this year, then runner-up in the Esports World Cup in Paris for $190,000.

He has also been improving over the board. He has climbed from under 2600 to 2650 on the classical list and says he performed around 2750 in three classical events this year. Chess.com's stats team made him the favorite and predicted 3+1 as his best segment, though not by this margin.

Lazavik has a preference for the next round. "I think Wesley is a small favorite in general, so I would prefer to play against Pragg, and usually I do quite well against Indian players," he said, citing wins over GMs Arjun Erigaisi and Nihal Sarin. He joins GM Hikaru Nakamura, who [beat Erdogmus 12-8](/articles/nakamura-wins-12-8-despite-losing-bullet-to-erdogmus-15/), in the Quarterfinals.

Quarterfinal winners earn a place in the Semifinals in London. The next Round of 16 match, GM Alireza Firouzja against GM Vladislav Artemiev, is on Friday, November 27 at 12:30 p.m. ET.

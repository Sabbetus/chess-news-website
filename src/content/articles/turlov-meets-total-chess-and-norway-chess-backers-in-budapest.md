FIDE President Timur Turlov [spent his visit to Budapest in a string of meetings](https://www.fide.com/fide-president-timur-turlov-holds-meetings-in-budapest/) after attending the opening ceremony of the Total Chess Pilot 2026 tournament. FIDE says the talks centered on expanding international cooperation and developing new areas of its work.

The list of people he met is the most telling part. It runs from a university rector to the organizers of a privately run chess tour, and it shows FIDE looking well beyond the board.

**Education and sports management**

Turlov met Tamás Sterbenz, Rector of the Hungarian University of Sports Science. They discussed cooperation in sports education, including specialized programs with FIDE's participation.

Special attention went to training specialists in sports management. The sides also considered a separate educational program tailored to the international chess community, and both expressed interest in continuing the dialogue.

A program like that would address a practical gap. Chess federations and event organizers need administrators as much as they need arbiters and coaches, and FIDE has few ready-made routes for producing them. That is my reading, not something FIDE has said.

**Paralympics and schools**

At a meeting with László Szabó, President of the Hungarian Paralympic Committee, the two sides discussed how to promote chess further in Paralympic sports. One idea was to use European sporting events as a platform for developing this area.

Turlov also met Gábor Pósfai, Hungary's Minister of Internal Affairs, on school chess. He stressed FIDE's interest in expanding educational opportunities and building chess programs for schoolchildren.

**The Total Chess side**

The commercial conversations were with Kjell Madland, founder of Norway Chess and the Total Chess World Championship Tour. Also present were Roy-André Tollefsen, Chairman of the Board of Directors of Total Chess, and Rune Hauge, a sports and media industry representative.

They exchanged views on international chess projects and possible formats of cooperation. FIDE stresses that the discussions were preliminary and aimed only at exploring opportunities.

That caution is worth respecting. Total Chess has drawn big names to Budapest, including [Carlsen, who won its blitz Swiss on 7/9](/articles/carlsen-wins-the-total-chess-blitz-swiss-on-7-9-unbeaten/).

Whether the federation and a privately run tour end up working together, or simply keep a line open, is not something the meeting settles. Our [guide to the Budapest pilot's format](/articles/total-chess-budapest-pilot-how-the-format-works/) explains what the tour is trying out.

**Other meetings**

Turlov also met Anel Bakytbekkyzy, the Ambassador of Kazakhstan to Hungary. He held further meetings with representatives of national chess federations from several European countries, though FIDE names none of them.

No agreements were announced from any of these meetings. The one concrete next step on record is the continued dialogue with the Hungarian University of Sports Science.

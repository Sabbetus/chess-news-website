The second half of ChessBase's [review of Robert Ris' FritzTrainers](https://en.chessbase.com/post/review-robert-ris-fritztrainers-on-openings-calculation-and-strategic-thinking-part-2) turns to his opening catalogue. The reviewer's central point is that the Dutch International Master (born 1988) teaches ideas before lines. That shapes how a buyer should read the list.

**Grammar before vocabulary**

*Mastering Pattern Recognition in the Opening* (2019) covers ten universal themes: the centre, lead in development, the king in the centre, neglected kingside development, poisoned pawns, captures towards the centre, premature castling, flank attacks, misplaced pieces and space advantage. It adds more than fifty interactive exercises and a database of illustrative games. The review calls it a kind of grammar of the opening.

*Basic Opening Strategy* (2023) goes lower still. In just over four hours it covers the golden rules, development, king safety, the early queen and the weakness of the f-pawn, with fifty exercises so the rules don't harden into dogma. The reviewer rates it for beginners and lower-rated club players, and says its strength is method rather than original insight: rule, then exception, then the specific position.

**The 1.d4 side**

*A Complete Black Repertoire against 1.d4* is built on the Queen's Gambit Accepted. It offers answers to the Trompowsky, London, Jobava, Torre, Colle, Veresov and Blackmar-Diemer. The package runs to around seven hours of video, twenty exercises, ten training positions and more than 150 model games.

The reviewer finds the QGA choice convincing: Black develops freely but has to challenge the centre. That matches what the review describes as Ris's habit of pairing solid openings with an active mindset.

The 60-minute courses are narrower. One examines the central QGA line with 3.e4 b5!?, another takes the Budapest Gambit into Fajarowicz territory, and a third gives White the Fianchetto Variation against the Semi-Slav. The reviewer notes the hour limits how fully the Semi-Slav main lines can be covered.

For White, the standout is *The Menacing Colle & Zuckertort System for the Club Player*. It needs little theory and is organised around key themes and typical plans in each structure Black can choose. The reviewer calls it one of Ris's best, and says *The Top-Tier Trompowsky* covers the setups that arise after 1...Nf6.

**The 1.e4 side**

Black's Sicilian is covered by *The Flexible Taimanov Sicilian* and *A Complete Guide for Black against the Anti-Sicilian*, both from 2024. The reviewer praises the second for giving the Alapin, Closed Sicilian and Grand Prix Attack a full course. Club players meet those lines more often than main-line coverage suggests.

Ris also has full-length courses on 1...e5 and the Open Games and on the Scandinavian, plus a newly released Accelerated Dragon course that ChessBase reviews separately. The 60-minute French, Philidor and Delayed Schliemann courses are called the experimental side: surprise weapons and blitz ideas, not foundations for a long-term repertoire.

**The shelf-life warning**

The reviewer likes the exercises and model games because they force players to judge key positions without a commentator. The caveat is age. Courses from 2015 or 2019 may still teach well, but specific variations, the Meran Tactic Toolbox included, can be outdated.

The advice is to treat each course as an explained framework and refresh it from current databases. Players unwilling to do that work are steered toward solid systems rather than the sharpest Sicilian lines.

For readers who like the approach, Ris's Sicilian play is not unique in drawing attention to offbeat ideas; [Adams's 3.Bc4 against the Sicilian](/articles/adams-plays-3-bc4-against-the-sicilian-and-nobody-laughs/) was a recent example of a sideline getting a serious hearing.

The Atyrau Masters, the third stage of the Kazakhstan Chess Cup, lost its two highest-rated players within two rounds. [Vietnamese GM Nguyen Duc Hoa (2405) beat top seed Shamsiddin Vokhidov (2649) in round one](https://en.chessbase.com/post/atyrau-masters-2026-r3), and Armenian IM Sergey Sklokin-Bagiyan (2477) took down second seed Leon Luke Mendonca (2629) in round two. In both rounds the favourite lost to someone rated more than 150 Elo points lower.

**How the seeds fell**

Vokhidov's loss came after he had done most of the hard work. He reached an objectively drawn opposite-coloured-bishops endgame with equal material, then [made an error that let Nguyen win](#game-embed).

The rating gap was 244 points, which by the standard Elo curve means the favourite would normally be expected to score somewhere around 80 percent, so a loss is far from impossible but not common. Opposite-coloured bishops are famously drawish, which makes dropping the point from there all the more painful.

Mendonca's game was a different kind of story. He had survived a clearly inferior middlegame and restored equality, only to badly misjudge a simplification. That allowed Sklokin-Bagiyan, playing White, to get a new queen by force.

For both seeds the damage is mostly practical. In a nine-round Swiss, a zero in the first two rounds means facing lower-rated opponents for a while, and the pairing system will not give them a quick route back to the top boards. They would need to string together wins to return to prize contention.

**The leaders**

Two players are perfect after three rounds: India's Pranav Anand, the fourth seed (2606), and Azerbaijan's Khagan Ahmad, the 18th seed (2524). Ahmad's third-round win came against Sklokin-Bagiyan, so the man who beat Mendonca has now lost himself.

Thirteen players sit half a point back on 2.5/3. Among them are Arkadij Naiditsch (2580, playing for Bulgaria), Emin Ohanyan, Artiom Stribuk, Sergei Lobanov, Ivan Zemlyanskii and Bardiya Daneshvar. Several of the field's lower-rated IMs are in that group too, including Lou Yiping, Shete Sammed Jaykumar and Aradhya Garg.

Behind them on 2 points are Mendonca, third seed Aditya Mittal (2621) and Gata Kamsky (2592), who now plays for France. Another name on 2 points is FM Yernur Yerzhanuly, whose rating is just 2109. Sklokin-Bagiyan is also on 2, with Vokhidov further down the list.

**Format and prizes**

The event runs from 12 to 20 October in Atyrau, a city at the mouth of the Ural River on the Caspian Sea, between Europe and Asia. Masters and Open sections both play nine rounds, at 90 minutes per game with a 30-second increment from move one. The final round is on Tuesday 20 October.

Draws cannot be offered before move 30, a rule aimed at discouraging quick agreed draws. Games are broadcast with a 15-minute delay as a fair-play measure. The combined prize fund is about US$100,000, and the Masters winner receives 9 million Kazakhstani tenge, roughly US$20,000.

Six rounds remain. With 109 players and a field that has already been scrambled, the leaders will soon meet stronger opposition, and the half-point chasing pack gives the top boards plenty of room to change.

Javokhir Sindarov is the Uzbek grandmaster who will challenge Gukesh Dommaraju for the World Championship in November 2026. Two years ago he was a strong young grandmaster on the fringe of the elite. What changed was a run of results few players have ever strung together: the 2025 World Cup, the 2026 Candidates, and Olympiad gold at home in Samarkand.

He was born in Tashkent in December 2005 and learned the game at four and a half from his grandfather, Komil Sindarov, now a vice president of the Uzbek chess federation. The talent showed early. He became a grandmaster in 2018 at 12 years, 10 months and 8 days, at the time the second-youngest in history, and grew up alongside a rival of almost exactly his age, Nodirbek Abdusattorov. The two later became the core of the Uzbekistan team that won Olympiad gold in 2022.

For several years Sindarov was best known for upsets: knocking the eighth seed, Alireza Firouzja, out of the 2021 World Cup, and Maxime Vachier-Lagrave out of the 2023 edition. He crossed 2700 in 2024. In January 2025 he began working with coach Roman Vidonyak, and the results that followed moved him from dangerous outsider to contender.

Seeded 16th at the 2025 World Cup, he beat Wei Yi in the final on tiebreaks to become the event's youngest winner. At the Candidates in Cyprus in April 2026 he scored 10/14, the highest total in the tournament's current double round-robin format, and clinched first place with a round to spare. Five months later he was part of the Uzbekistan team that won the Olympiad again, this time in Samarkand.

His style is aggressive and dynamic, but his coach, Roman Vidonyak, points to something less flashy: fast, confident decisions and deep opening preparation, which let him build a clock advantage even in complicated positions. "If I get a dynamic position with any colour, I will be very happy," he said during the Candidates.
